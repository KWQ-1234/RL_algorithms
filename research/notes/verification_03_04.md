### RLOO equivalence (Dr. GRPO, App. A, "Unbiasedness of Ã_{i,t}")
Dr. GRPO's advantage is "equivalent to that of REINFORCE Leave-One-Out (RLOO)… up to a scaling factor". The derivation shows `G/(G−1) · Ã_{i,t} = Â^RLOO_{i,t}`. So the claim "RLOO advantage = Dr. GRPO advantage × G/(G−1)" is **VERIFIED**. The paper adds that the factor "can be subsumed into the learning rate".

### Verdict on the section's thesis
At r ≡ 1, every method above gives the per-token gradient `A_i · ∇log π · c_i`, where c_i is a normalizer: `1/(G|o_i|)` for GRPO, GSPO, GMPO and SAPO; `1/Σ|o_j|` for DAPO and CISPO; `1/G` or `1/(G·MAX_TOKENS)` for Dr. GRPO. **No method leaves a residual factor from its ratio, gate or clip.** SAPO's gate derivative is exactly 1, GSPO and GMPO multiply by a geometric mean that equals 1, and every clip interval contains 1. Four caveats the blog should state:
1. **The advantage still differs between methods.** GRPO, DAPO, GSPO, GMPO, SAPO and CISPO divide by the group std; Dr. GRPO does not (and RLOO rescales by G/(G−1)). So "differing only in normalization" is true only if "normalization" covers the advantage's std as well as the length or token normalizer.
2. **GRPO's KL term** adds `β(π_ref/π_θ − 1)` to the coefficient (DeepSeekMath Eq. 21). It does not vanish at r = 1. PPO's entropy bonus (Eq. 9) is the analogous residual.
3. **None of these papers trains strictly on-policy in its headline experiments.** DAPO does 16 gradient updates per rollout step (§4.1). MiniMax's setup has "16 rounds of off-policy updates per generation batch" (§3.1). GSPO and SAPO split each rollout batch into 4 mini-batches. GMPO does 8 updates per round (1,024 rollouts, batch 128). So r = 1 holds only for the first mini-batch.
4. **A train/inference mismatch makes r ≠ 1 even with a single update** if the ratio is taken against rollout-engine logprobs. MiniMax-M1 §3.2 reports this mismatch and its FP32 LM-head fix: correlation of train/inference probabilities went from "approximately 0.9x to 0.99x".

## Summary: WRONG / NOT FOUND / needs rewording

- **Nothing in Parts A or B is outright WRONG** as the claims were posed.
- **NOT FOUND:** a numeric value for CISPO's ε^IS_high in MiniMax-M1. The paper only says the lower bound is effectively disabled and ε^IS_high was tuned and later decreased.
- **NOT ESTABLISHED:** that WPO's DPO 20.6 and SimPO's DPO 15.1 come from the *same* SFT checkpoint. WPO uses `HuggingFaceH4/mistral-7b-sft-beta`. SimPO trains its own UltraChat-200k SFT, and the two SFT LC values differ (9.5 vs 8.4).
- **Reword:**
  1. PRIME's overhead: the text says 24%, Table 2 gives 28.2%.
  2. VC-PPO PPO = 5.6 (pass@1) vs VAPO vanilla PPO = 5 (avg@32). These are different papers and metric labels.
  3. ScaleRL's long-context run is labelled **34k** in Fig. 9 but "32k" in the text.
  4. ScaleRL contradicts itself: §3.1 says PipelineRL and PPO-off-policy reach similar A, while App. A.11 says the off-policy algorithm shifts the asymptote.
  5. Sober Look's "5–15 pp std" is spread across 20 seeds over all models and benchmarks. AIME24 specifically is "up to 15%".
  6. The ">0.99 R²" in 2610.04158 holds on average (Fig. 5 caption); per-benchmark R² is as low as about 0.85.
  7. Part A thesis: the gradients collapse to REINFORCE at r = 1. They still differ in the advantage (std vs no std; RLOO's G/(G−1)), and GRPO keeps a KL residual `β(π_ref/π_θ − 1)`. Also, none of the cited papers' own experiments are strictly on-policy.
