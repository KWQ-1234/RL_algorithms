# RL Algorithms for Agentic, Multi-turn, Tool-using LLMs: Which Changes Are Decisive?

Scope note: these notes cover algorithmic changes specific to multi-turn agent trajectories (search, code/TIR, SWE, web, embodied text games, GUI/VLM). Single-turn tricks (clip-higher, Dr.GRPO, token-level loss) appear only where an agent paper uses or tests them. Research date: 2026-10-14.

Reading depth legend (applies to every source): **[full]** = arXiv HTML or official page read in full or substantially (first ~100k chars of long papers; appendices sometimes truncated); **[abs]** = abstract page only; **[snippet]** = only a search-engine summary or secondary coverage. Claims from [abs] and [snippet] sources are flagged inline.

Evidence-grade convention: A = multiple independent controlled ablations; B = one controlled ablation (multi-seed or clean same-setup comparison, including in the originating paper) or a clear, checkable mechanism plus convergent adoption; C = only the originating paper's claim, no clean control (single run, mismatched baselines, or figure-only); D = contradictory evidence across sources. Verdict dimensions: **ceiling** (final performance), **efficiency** (samples/compute/tool calls to reach a level), **stability** (collapse avoidance), **side effects**.

## Q1. Which choices are specific to multi-turn (loss masking of environment tokens, turn-level advantages, trajectory filtering, length/turn budgets), and are they decisive or incidental?

### Takeaway
Only two multi-turn-specific choices have clean, same-setup evidence of large effects: (i) masking environment/tool tokens from the loss (about +9 EM points on Search-R1 7B; universally adopted) and (ii) dropping malformed/void trajectories in tool-integrated reasoning (SimpleTIR: AIME24 20.8 → 50.5, single runs). Turn-level/step-level advantage methods (GiGPO, MT-GRPO, LOOP's per-token ratio, VAGEN bi-level GAE, HCAPO) show +5–15 pp gains, but only on small models and short-horizon benchmarks (ALFWorld, WebShop, AppWorld, Sokoban), with baselines that were not tuned and no independent re-runs. No frontier lab reports using them. Masking truncated or timed-out trajectories and setting turn/context budgets are universal in practice, but I found no controlled ablation of either.

### Cited Findings

#### Method card: Environment/tool-token loss masking (+ token-in-token-out consistency)
- **(a) Formula:** I(y_t)=1 for LLM-generated tokens, 0 for retrieved tokens. Loss sums only over I(y_t)=1 and normalizes by the count of those tokens. Under GRPO the KL term is masked the same way. — [Search-R1, arXiv 2503.09516 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516)
- **(b) Mechanism:** Without masking, the policy gradient pushes up the likelihood of retriever/interpreter text the policy never sampled. That is behavior cloning on environment output, with importance ratios that have no on-policy meaning. ToRL states the motivation as stopping the model from "memorizing" execution outputs. — [ToRL, arXiv 2503.23383 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23383)
- **(c) Original evidence:** Search-R1, Qwen2.5-7B-base, PPO, single run per cell, 500 steps, NQ+HotpotQA training, 7 QA test sets: average EM 0.431 with the mask vs 0.343 without. Musique 0.196 vs 0.108; Bamboogle 0.432 vs 0.304. Qwen2.5-3B-base PPO: 0.303 vs 0.262, but unmasked is slightly *higher* on Musique (0.053 vs 0.049) and Bamboogle (0.104 vs 0.088). No seeds or variance are reported. — [Search-R1 2503.09516 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516)
- **(d) Independent evidence:** I found no second controlled masking ablation. Everyone adopts it with no ablation: R1-Searcher ([2503.05592 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05592)), ToRL, ReTool ([2504.11536 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11536)), WebSailor ([2507.02592 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02592)), WebDancer ([2505.22648 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22648)), GLM-4.5 ("the environment feedback is ignored in loss computation", [2508.06471 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06471)), GLM-5 ([2602.15763 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15763)), verl agent loop (`response_mask` is 1 for LLM tokens and 0 for tool tokens; [verl docs agent_loop.rst](https://github.com/volcengine/verl/blob/main/docs/advance/agent_loop.rst), read from local clone). The Practitioner's Guide masks state tokens but does not ablate it ([2510.01132 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01132)).
- **Companion requirement, token-in-token-out (TITO):** verl reports that re-applying `apply_chat_template` to the final message list does not reproduce the concatenated per-turn token IDs. Two causes: tool parsers strip `<tool_call>` text, and decode→encode is not round-trip safe. They say this "make[s] PPO training not even converged in single-turn" ([verl agent_loop.rst](https://github.com/volcengine/verl/blob/main/docs/advance/agent_loop.rst)). GLM-5 added a "token-in-token-out gateway" that records token IDs directly to avoid re-tokenization mismatch ([GLM-5 2602.15763 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15763)).
- **(e) Verdict:** Ceiling: large (+0.088 average EM at 7B; smaller and mixed at 3B). Efficiency: n/a. Stability: likely positive by mechanism, but untested directly. Side effects: none reported. **Grade B** (one controlled ablation plus a clear mechanism plus universal adoption). TITO consistency is also **grade B**: practitioner reports and a clear mechanism, no controlled table. Both are correctness requirements, not tunable algorithms.

#### Method card: MT-GRPO / turn-level credit assignment ("Reinforcing Multi-Turn Reasoning in LLM Agents via Turn-Level Credit Assignment")
- **(a) Formula:** A^{MT}_{i,(k)} = Σ_{l=k}^{K−1} α^{l−k} A^I_{i,(l)} + α^{K−k} A^O_i. A^I is the group-normalized intermediate (turn) advantage computed from G actions sampled at the same state. A^O is the group-normalized outcome (exact-match) advantage. No α value is given. — [arXiv 2505.11821 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11821)
- **(b) Mechanism:** Verifiable per-turn rewards (tool executed, format, search answer) get credited to the turn that earned them rather than smeared across the trajectory.
- **(c) Original evidence:** Qwen2.5-7B, TriviaQA, Wikipedia search, **exactly 2 turns**. Validation EM: GRPO with outcome-only reward 0, GRPO with merged rewards 0.3346, MT-GRPO 0.5010. Training curves average 10 runs. The authors note the cost is G^{K−1} trajectories, "computationally prohibitive for long-horizon", and that every group must have the same number of turns. — [2505.11821 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11821)
- **(d) Independent evidence:** none found that re-runs MT-GRPO. Their "outcome-only GRPO = 0 EM" baseline contradicts Search-R1, where outcome-only GRPO/PPO on the same model family reaches 0.35–0.43 average EM ([2503.09516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516)). The baseline failure therefore looks setup-specific (prompt/format), not inherent to outcome reward.
- **(e) Verdict:** Ceiling: unproven beyond 2 turns. Efficiency: negative at scale (exponential rollouts). Stability: claimed lower variance, qualitative only. Side effects: requires hand-designed verifiable turn rewards. **Grade C.**

#### Method card: GiGPO (Group-in-Group Policy Optimization; anchor-state grouping)
- **(a) Formula:** A = A^E + ω·A^S. A^E is the episode return normalized within the group, (R − mean)/F_norm, where F_norm is the std or 1 (F_norm=1 is RLOO-equivalent up to scale). A^S groups identical environment states across the group's trajectories ("anchor states", found by hashmap lookup). Each action at that state gets discounted return R_t = Σ_{k≥t} γ^{k−t} r_k (γ=0.95), normalized within the state group. ω=1. For search QA, "identical" is relaxed to a longest-matching-subsequence ratio > 0.9. — [arXiv 2505.10978 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10978)
- **(b) Mechanism:** Repeated states within a group give a free Monte-Carlo step-level baseline with no critic and no extra rollouts.
- **(c) Original evidence:** Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct; ALFWorld (≤50 steps), WebShop (≤15 steps); group size 8 × 16 groups; 150 iterations; lr 1e-6; KL 0.01; **3 seeds** for Table 1; identical hyperparameters across RL methods, with no separate baseline tuning. ALFWorld 1.5B: GiGPO 86.7 / GRPO 72.8±3.6 / RLOO 69.7 / PPO 54.4. ALFWorld 7B: 90.8 / 77.6 / 75.5 / **PPO 80.4**. WebShop success 1.5B: 67.4±4.5 (no std) / GRPO 56.8±3.8 / PPO 51.5. WebShop 7B: 75.2 / GRPO 66.1 / PPO 68.7. Search QA average: 3B 42.1 vs Search-R1 32.5; 7B 47.2 vs 38.5 (no seeds). ω sweep (WebShop 1.5B, single runs): ω=0 → 76.2, ω=0.8 → 84.9, ω=1.0 → 83.5. Removing the step term causes "pronounced declines" (figure only). Overhead: 0.54 s out of 362.83 s per iteration ≈ 0.15%, which is about 75× more than the paper's own "<0.002%" claim (my arithmetic from their numbers; small either way). — [2505.10978 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10978)
- **(d) Independent evidence:** HCAPO ([arXiv 2603.08754 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08754)) reports GiGPO 86.7/90.8 (ALFWorld) and 65.0/72.8 (WebShop). Those values are **copied from the GiGPO paper, not re-run**, and HCAPO beats them by only 0.3–3.5 pp (e.g., ALFWorld 7B 91.4 vs 90.8). Role-Agent ([2606.10917 [snippet]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10917)) lists GiGPO 42.1 on 3B search QA, identical to the GiGPO paper's number, so also likely copied. TAPO ([2607.27973 [snippet]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27973)) stacks on top of GiGPO. I found **no independent re-run** of GiGPO, and no study of anchor-state repeat rates in open-ended environments.
- **(e) Verdict:** Ceiling: +9–14 pp over GRPO in ALFWorld/WebShop at ≤7B (3 seeds, so the margin exceeds the ±3–4 std). Efficiency: same rollout budget. Stability: not separately characterized. Side effects: the gain depends on exact state repetition. ALFWorld/WebShop are deterministic with small discrete state spaces. In SWE or web browsing, observations almost never repeat exactly, so A^S degenerates toward zero and GiGPO reduces to GRPO/RLOO (inference; the authors already needed a fuzzy-match heuristic for search). **Grade B−** (one controlled multi-seed comparison in the originating paper; untuned baselines; no independent replication).

#### Method card: LOOP (leave-one-out PPO, no critic) and importance-ratio granularity
- **(a) Formula:** A(c,x_k) = K/(K−1)·(R_k − (1/K)Σ_i R_i), with K=6. PPO clip written as g_ε(A)=A+ε|A|. Per-token importance weights averaged over LLM-generated tokens. No value network, one model copy. — [arXiv 2502.01600 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01600)
- **(c) Original evidence:** AppWorld (9 apps, 457 APIs); Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct + LoRA r=16; 24 scenarios / 72 tasks; reward = fraction of unit tests passed; up to 40 interactions; about 530 steps; 2×8 H100 for 42 h. Best-run Test-N TGC: LOOP token 71.3, LOOP turn 64.1, LOOP trajectory ("bandit") 53.3, GRPO 58.0, GRPO no-KL 59.0, RLOO trajectory 57.2, PPO with learned critic 50.8, o1 61.9. Reward normalization (std division) costs about 9 pp; KL costs about 2 pp. **Table 2 averages 3 training runs:** LOOP token 66.4±4.8 Test-N, 41.7±3.4 Test-C, still the top method. Trajectory-level weights trained unstably. — [2502.01600 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01600)
- **Behavioral side effects (positive):** multiple code cells per turn fell about 6×, API doc lookups rose about 1.6×, "assuming" fell about 30×, giving up after an API error fell about 3×. — [2502.01600 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01600)
- **(d) Independent / contradictory evidence on ratio granularity:** SORL ([arXiv 2511.20718 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20718)) argues the **opposite**: token-level ratios are unstable off-policy, and a **turn-level** ratio (the geometric mean of the turn's token ratios) plus clipping-triggered gradient normalization (divide by max(‖Ĉ_turn‖, 1)) prevents collapse. Medical search QA, Llama-3.1-8B: Search-R1 PPO 45.37 vs SO-PPO 53.88 vs SO-GRPO 55.34. On NQ, token-level PPO collapsed at about steps 250–300, and GRPO and GSPO also collapsed with KL spikes. The printed Eq. 4 appears to square the geometric mean (likely a typo). Fig. 5 uses 3 trials; other comparisons are single runs. AT2PO ([2601.04767 [snippet]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04767)) also argues neither token nor sequence ratios fit the turn-wise sampling process.
- **(e) Verdict:** LOOP's critic-free, leave-one-out baseline without std normalization is well supported: three seeds, and it is consistent with DeepSWE/SkyRL-Agent adopting LOO without std normalization. **Grade B.** Per-token vs per-turn vs per-sequence ratio is **Grade D**. LOOP (on-policy-ish, LoRA, 32B) finds per-token best; SORL (off-policy minibatches, search) finds per-turn more stable. The two regimes differ in off-policyness, which is the likely reconciling variable (inference).

#### Method card: VAGEN Bi-Level GAE (multi-turn PPO with critic) + world-model reward
- **(a) Formula:** Turn-level TD error δ_t^turn = r_t + γ_turn V(τ̄≤a_{t+1}) − V(τ̄≤a_t), with turn-level GAE. Token-level GAE runs within each action. The last token of action a_t is initialized with A_t^turn. Turn reward r_t = r^reason + r^format + R(s_t,a_t), where r^reason = 0.5·I[StateEstimation] + 0.5·I[TransitionModeling], judged by an LLM. γ/λ values are not given. — [arXiv 2510.16907 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16907)
- **(c) Original evidence:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B, 5 visual environments (Sokoban, FrozenLake, Navigation, PrimitiveSkill, SVG). Overall success: untrained 0.21, vanilla PPO 0.26, GRPO w/ mask 0.54, Turn-PPO w/ mask 0.55, VAGEN-Base (structured world-model reasoning format, token GAE) 0.76, VAGEN-Full (+WM reward +Bi-Level GAE) 0.82. Steps and seeds are not stated in the text read. — [2510.16907 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16907)
- **(d) Independent:** none found.
- **(e) Verdict:** Most of the gain (0.55 → 0.76) comes from the **reasoning format/prompt structure**, not the credit-assignment algorithm (+0.06). The authors report **reward hacking**: with Bi-Level GAE plus the LLM-judge WM reward, agents produced generic, state-agnostic text that satisfied the judge. Masking explains 0.26 → 0.54/0.55. **Grade C** for bi-level GAE; the masking effect corroborates the Search-R1 masking card.

#### Method card: ArCHer (hierarchical: utterance-level off-policy critic + token-level policy)
- **(a)/(b):** A high-level off-policy value-based learner aggregates reward over utterances, and a low-level token policy is trained against that value function. — [arXiv 2402.19446 [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19446)
- **(c):** The abstract claims "about 100x" sample efficiency over existing methods, with scaling up to 7B. The environments (WebShop, Twenty Questions, Detective) are from my memory of the paper, **not verified (abstract only)**.
- **(d):** No 2025–26 frontier lab or major open agent stack (verl, SkyRL, rLLM, slime, AReaL) reports using an off-policy utterance-level critic. VAGEN's turn-level GAE is the nearest descendant.
- **(e) Verdict:** **Grade C** (abstract-level reading; not adopted at scale).

#### Method card: Practitioner's Guide to Multi-turn Agentic RL (algorithm × reward density × SFT budget)
- **(c) Evidence:** TextWorld, Qwen-1.5B, w2-o3-q4: PPO 0.88, RLOO 0.51, GRPO 0.18, Reinforce++ 0.18. On the harder w4-o6-q8: PPO 0.59, all others ≤0.02. At 7B the gap narrows (w2-o3-q4: PPO 0.98, RLOO 0.97, GRPO 0.79). Reward density with PPO is **non-monotonic**: sparse 0.41, dense-1 0.29, dense-2 0.58, and the text misstates this. Budget split (SFT demos at 10× episode cost): 60 demos + 400 RL episodes = 0.85, near SFT-only 100 demos = 0.95. Only PPO was tuned (Appendix sweep); GRPO, RLOO and Reinforce++ used fixed defaults; no seeds. — [arXiv 2510.01132v2 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01132)
- **Verdict:** These are the largest algorithm deltas in the literature, but they come from small models on text games, and the critic method was the only one tuned. **Grade C** for "PPO ≫ GRPO in multi-turn".

#### Method card: Turn/length budgets and trajectory filtering (truncation masking, "compact filtering")
- **DeepSWE "compact filtering":** mask the loss for trajectories that hit max context, time out during generation (20 min), or reach max steps. It is part of "GRPO++" = clip-high + no KL + no reward std + length normalization by max context + leave-one-out + compact filtering + no entropy loss. Qwen3-32B, about 4.5K R2E-Gym problems, 200 steps: SWE-bench Verified 23 → 42.2% Pass@1 (average of 16 runs), 59% with hybrid test-time scaling (Best@16). **No per-component ablation published.** — [DeepSWE-Preview model card [full]](https://huggingface.co/agentica-org/DeepSWE-Preview) (the Notion blog did not render for me).
- **SkyRL-Agent SA-SWE-32B:** trajectories cut off by context or step limits are excluded from gradients, but their rewards still count in the advantages (other samples' baselines). LOO advantage, no std or length normalization, no KL or entropy loss, fully on-policy, 125 steps, 32K context, 50 train steps / 100 eval steps. 24.4 → 39.4% Pass@1. The stated rationale is to avoid biasing the model against long trajectories. No ablation. — [arXiv 2511.16108 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16108)
- **Tongyi DeepResearch:** excludes negatives that hit the length limit without a final answer, because "unfiltered negatives caused degradation and possible policy collapse" (qualitative, no numbers). — [arXiv 2510.24701 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24701)
- **Kimi-Researcher:** "negative sample control" discards some negatives to reduce entropy-collapse risk (no numbers). — [Kimi-Researcher blog [full]](https://moonshotai.github.io/Kimi-Researcher/)
- **GLM-5:** excludes samples that failed because of environment collapse. A group is padded with repeated valid samples if valid samples exceed half the group, otherwise dropped. — [2602.15763 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15763)
- **Turn budget effects:** Practitioner's Guide: TextWorld PPO with 6/8/12/16 steps → 0.55/0.73/0.80/0.88; SWE-Gym GRPO with 10/25/40 tool calls → 0.03/0.18/0.22 (25 test instances). — [2510.01132 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01132). ToRL: tool budget C=1 → 2 gives about +2% average, but step time is 118 s (C=0), 237 s (C=1), 288 s (C=2) on 8×A800. — [2503.23383 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23383). ASearcher argues ≤10-turn limits block strategy learning and uses 32 turns (7B/14B) and 128 (QwQ), but has **no direct 10-vs-128 ablation**. — [arXiv 2508.07976 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07976)
- **Verdict:** Masking truncated/timeout trajectories: **Grade C** as a measured effect (zero ablations), though the mechanism is clear and adoption converges (DeepSWE, SkyRL-Agent, Tongyi, Kimi, GLM-5, DeepSeek-V3.2's related negative-advantage masking). Turn-budget size: **Grade B** for "larger budget raises ceiling in small settings", with a monotonic efficiency cost.

### Inferences
- The decisive multi-turn-specific choices are **correctness constraints** (mask env tokens; keep token IDs consistent; don't train on trajectories whose failure the environment or the budget caused). The credit-assignment "algorithms" are second-order outside short-horizon, repeat-state benchmarks.
- Turn-level advantage methods require either repeated states (GiGPO), fixed turn counts and branching (MT-GRPO, G^{K−1}), a learned critic (VAGEN, ArCHer), or a judge. Each requirement breaks in long-horizon SWE/web settings. This plausibly explains why frontier labs use trajectory-level group baselines plus reward-to-go and process rewards (MiniMax) rather than these methods.
- Truncation masking interacts with length incentives. If truncated negatives are dropped, nothing penalizes running out the budget, so labs pair it with explicit budget/efficiency terms: Kimi γ-decay, MiniMax speed reward, Kimi K2 truncation penalty, K2.5 Toggle (see Q3/Q4).

### Gaps
- No controlled ablation of compact filtering / truncation masking on any SWE or search benchmark.
- No independent re-implementation of GiGPO, MT-GRPO, VAGEN or ArCHer with tuned baselines, and no measurement of anchor-state repeat rates outside ALFWorld/WebShop.
- ArCHer, EMPG ([2509.09265 [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09265)), Tree-GRPO ([2509.21240 [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21240)), AEPO ([2510.14545 [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14545)) and Context-Folding ([2510.11967 [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11967)) were read at abstract level only. Their numbers are not in these notes.

## Q2. Which instabilities are specific to agentic RL (distribution shift from tool outputs, low-probability tokens from external content, collapse / "Echo Trap"), and which fixes have controlled evidence?

### Takeaway
Multi-turn GRPO/PPO collapse is replicated across independent groups: RAGEN, Search-R1, the Search-R1 empirical study, SORL, SimpleTIR. Each group names a different mechanism:
- RAGEN: reward-variance collapse ("Echo Trap").
- SimpleTIR: low-probability tokens after OOD tool output that explode gradients.
- SORL: high-variance ratios on format tokens under off-policy updates.
- ARPO: entropy spikes after tool calls.

The only fix with a large same-setup effect is SimpleTIR's void-turn trajectory filtering. It beat low-prob filtering and high-ratio filtering head-to-head, but in single runs reporting peak scores. Everything else is grade C. The frontier-lab consensus fix is behavior-proxy importance correction and masking against rollout log-probs, and it has no published ablation numbers.

### Cited Findings

#### Echo Trap / StarPO-S (RAGEN)
- **(a) Formula:** StarPO optimizes J = E[R(τ)] over whole trajectories, with the trajectory advantage shared by all its tokens (PPO or GRPO). StarPO-S adds three things: keep the top p% of prompts by within-group reward std (default 25%); keep the PPO critic; and remove KL plus decoupled clip (ε_low 0.2, ε_high 0.28). — [RAGEN arXiv 2504.20073 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20073)
- **(b) Echo Trap symptoms:** reward std collapses first, then entropy drops, then gradient-norm spikes mark irreversible collapse. Reasoning becomes templated ("choosing Dragon"). PPO timelines: FrozenLake std drop at step 40 and reward collapse at 90. Sokoban std and mean collapse together near step 10. Gradient spikes at 170 (Bandit), 110 (Sokoban), 90 (FrozenLake). — [2504.20073 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20073)
- **(c) Evidence:** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (toy envs), 3B (WebShop). P=8 prompts × N=16 rollouts, ≤5 turns, 100–200 iterations, seeds not stated. Retaining 75% of prompts extends FrozenLake PPO stability from 100 to 140 steps; 50% "avoids collapse entirely". KL removal and clip-higher are qualitative only. Reasoning traces shrink in multi-turn (Sokoban 307 → 89.5 tokens), and removing `<think>` "often yields comparable results". — [2504.20073 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20073)
- **(d) Independent:** RAGEN-2 (template collapse) reports that entropy can stay stable while reasoning becomes input-agnostic, and proposes mutual information as a better proxy. **[snippet only; arXiv ID unverified]**, via [catalyzex author listing](https://www.catalyzex.com/author/Qineng%20Wang). Dynamic sampling and zero-variance filtering are independently adopted: DAPO-style in WebDancer ([2505.22648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22648)), ASearcher ([2508.07976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07976)), WebSailor DUPO, and GLM-5 group padding.
- **(e) Verdict:** The phenomenon (reward-variance collapse preceding entropy collapse) is well described. The **top-p% variance filter as a fix** is **Grade C**: one paper, toy environments, 0.5B, no seeds, step-of-collapse as the metric. Removing zero-variance groups is **Grade B** by mechanism, since such groups contribute exactly zero GRPO gradient and only dilute the batch.

#### SimpleTIR void-turn filtering (TIR instability)
- **(a) Rule:** a void turn is a turn producing "neither a code block nor a final answer" (partial code, repetition, premature EOS). If any turn in a trajectory is void, the **entire trajectory** is removed from the GRPO update. — [arXiv 2509.02479 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02479)
- **(b) Mechanism (Prop. 3.1):** the logit-gradient norm grows with (i) importance ratios unbounded above on negatively-rewarded trajectories and (ii) low-probability sampled tokens under an otherwise sharp distribution. OOD tool output raises the chance of sampling such tokens, and this "compounds over successive turns". — [2509.02479 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02479)
- **(c) Evidence:** Qwen2.5-7B/32B base (zero RL), 5 turns/16K growing to 10 turns/24K. Batch 512, 16 rollouts, clip 0.2/0.28, grad-clip 1, no KL or entropy. Ablation (7B, **peak** within 1000 steps): SimpleTIR AIME24 50.5 / MATH500 88.4; naive multi-turn 20.8/73.1; low-prob-token filtering 23.3/72.8; high-ratio filtering 26.3/75.0; stop-generation without filtering 26.1/77.3. **No seeds, no variance.** Peak-within-window reporting was chosen because the ablated runs were unstable. — [2509.02479 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02479)
- **(d) Independent:** none found that replicates void-turn filtering. Cross-paper numbers are not controlled: SimpleTIR's table lists ARPO-7B AIME24 30.0 and ToRL-7B 40.2 under different setups.
- **(e) Verdict:** Ceiling: very large in this setup (+30 AIME24 points over naive). Stability: the main purpose (gradient norms stay bounded per Fig. 5). Side effects: biases training toward well-formed trajectories and discards learning signal from malformed failures. Generic token-level filters (low-prob, high-ratio) did **not** substitute for it, which is the most informative result. **Grade B−/C** (one same-setup ablation, single runs, peak metric).

#### ARPO / AEPO (entropy-guided branching after tool calls)
- **(a) ARPO rule:** ΔH_t = Normalize(H_t − H_initial) over the first k tokens after a tool response; branch probability P_t = α + β·ΔH_t; branch Z times if P_t > τ. The budget is M = N global + (M−N) partial samples. Advantage attribution: "soft" (default, plain GRPO ratio, so shared-prefix tokens get aligned updates implicitly) or "hard" (shared tokens get the mean advantage of the d sharing trajectories). — [arXiv 2507.19849 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19849)
- **(b) Observation:** entropy "rises sharply in the first 10–50 tokens following each tool call", and more after search than after Python. No numeric entropy values are given. — [2507.19849 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19849)
- **(c) Evidence:** Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Llama3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B/14B; "13 benchmarks" (inconsistent with tables saying 10). Claims GRPO-level results with "half the number of tool calls" (Fig. 7, no absolute counts). **No ablation isolating branching vs attribution vs plain GRPO at equal budget; no seeds;** α, β, τ, Z, k defaults not given. — [2507.19849 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19849)
- **AEPO [abs only]:** dynamic entropy-balanced rollout with a penalty on consecutive high-entropy branching, stop-gradient on the high-entropy clipping term, and entropy-aware advantage. Qwen3-14B with 1K RL samples: GAIA 47.6 Pass@1, HLE 11.2, WebWalker 43.0. Claims to beat "7 mainstream RL algorithms" (unnamed). — [arXiv 2510.14545 [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14545)
- **(e) Verdict:** Efficiency is plausible by construction, because shared-prefix branching samples more leaves per tool call (cf. Tree-GRPO [abs], MiniMax prefix-tree merging). Ceiling and stability are unproven. **Grade C.** Note the opposite reactions to the same post-tool entropy spike: ARPO exploits it (branch there), SimpleTIR treats the resulting low-probability tokens as the instability source. Neither paper tests the other's hypothesis.

#### Off-policy / training–inference mismatch fixes as used for agents
- **CISPO (MiniMax-M1):** J = (1/Σ|o_i|) Σ_i Σ_t sg(r̂_{i,t}) Â_{i,t} log π_θ(o_{i,t}), with r̂ = clip(r, 1−ε_low^IS, 1+ε_high^IS). In practice there is no lower bound ("we did not impose a lower bound") and no KL. Clipped tokens still contribute gradient, through the log π term weighted by the clipped ratio. Evidence: Qwen2.5-32B-base zero-RL **math** (not agentic), matching DAPO with 50% of the steps. Other M1 fixes: an fp32 LM head raised train/infer probability correlation from about 0.9x to 0.99x; generation is halted if 3,000 consecutive tokens each have p > 0.99. — [arXiv 2506.13585 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13585). MiniMax keeps CISPO for M2.5 agent RL with clip(r, 0, 1+ε_high) — [Forge blog [full]](https://www.minimax.io/blog/forge-scalable-agent-rl-en-1779896141); [MiniMax-M2 series report 2605.26494 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26494). **Grade C for agentic use** (the only controlled comparison is math).
- **SORL turn-level IS + clipping-triggered normalization:** see the LOOP card. **Grade C.** Off-policyness was manipulated only through the minibatch count.
- **DeepSeek-V3.2:** (1) unbiased KL (K3 estimator corrected by the IS ratio); (2) **off-policy sequence masking**: M_{i,t}=0 if Â<0 **and** the mean old/current log-ratio exceeds δ, i.e. "only mask sequences with negative advantages"; (3) Keep Routing (reuse sampling-time MoE routes in training); (4) Keep Sampling Mask (reapply top-p/top-k truncation masks in training). **No ablation numbers in the text.** — [arXiv 2512.02556 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556)
- **Kimi K2.5:** a token-level clip zeros the gradient for tokens whose log-ratio falls outside [α, β] "regardless of the sign of the advantages", targeting train/infer mismatch. No values or ablations. — [arXiv 2602.02276 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02276)
- **GLM-5:** uses rollout log-probs as the behavior proxy (r = exp(log π_θ − log π_rollout)) and fully masks tokens outside [1−ε_l, 1+ε_h]. Discards samples whose oldest rollout version is too stale. Resets the optimizer after each inference-weight update. No ablation numbers. — [2602.15763 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15763)
- **Reasoning-model collapse under GRPO in search:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B under GRPO averages 0.100 EM vs 0.420 for Qwen2.5-7B-Base. Under PPO the R1-distill model reaches 0.344 (slow but stable). The authors attribute the collapse to GRPO's higher variance and to the reasoning model's poor instruction-following (rarely searches early). — [arXiv 2505.15117 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15117). Search-R1 also reports GRPO "reward collapse" after many steps while PPO stays stable (curves only). — [2503.09516 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516)
- **Environment-induced instability:** SkyRL-Agent's deep-research run dipped around iteration 30 because an under-provisioned summarizer timed out and contaminated rollouts. Fixes were more tool capacity plus masking abnormal trajectories. Answer leakage from Hugging Face/GitHub pages was fixed by blocking those domains. — [2511.16108 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16108). Tongyi: "unstable tools can contaminate trajectories", hence QPS limits, caching, retry, failover, and an offline Wikipedia simulator whose reward curve tracks the real environment. — [2510.24701 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24701). DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash reports agents attempting reward hacking or crashing environments during RL. — [arXiv 2609.19969 [snippet]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969)

### Inferences
- "Agent-specific instability" is mostly the single-turn off-policy/low-probability-token problem, made worse in three ways: (i) tool outputs push the context OOD, raising entropy and the rate of low-probability samples; (ii) long, variable-length trajectories force asynchronous, stale rollouts (Q3); (iii) environment failures inject reward noise uncorrelated with the policy. The fixes that labs actually ship target (i)+(ii) with ratio masking/clipping against rollout log-probs, and (iii) with environment engineering plus abnormal-trajectory masking. Neither has a published ablation.
- PPO-with-critic vs GRPO is **Grade D**:
  - Critic wins: Search-R1 7B-base (0.431 vs 0.350); Practitioner's Guide TextWorld (only PPO tuned); GiGPO's PPO beating GRPO at 7B on ALFWorld (80.4 vs 77.6); the R1-distill collapse.
  - Critic loses: LOOP AppWorld (PPO 50.8 vs GRPO 58.0); GiGPO 1.5B (PPO 54.4 vs GRPO 72.8); Search-R1 3B and 7B-instruct (GRPO ≥ PPO).
  - The pattern suggests critics help most when group variance is high or groups are small (GRPO size 5 in Search-R1), not universally.

### Gaps
- No controlled study compares DeepSeek's negative-advantage sequence masking, GLM-5's ratio masking, K2.5's sign-agnostic token clip, CISPO and SORL on the same agentic task.
- The RAGEN-2 "template collapse" result was seen only via a secondary listing; its arXiv ID and details are unverified.
- No paper measures how often post-tool-output tokens are the clipped or high-ratio tokens in large-scale agent runs. SORL's observation (clipped tokens are mostly format symbols) comes from a single run.

## Q3. Does plain GRPO/PPO with outcome reward work fine for agents, with the "algorithm" contributions mostly being data/env engineering? Quantify.

### Takeaway
Mostly yes. Every strong open SWE or search agent result in 2025–26 uses a trajectory-level group baseline (GRPO/RLOO/REINFORCE), binary or near-binary outcome reward, env-token masking, and a few single-turn stabilizers. The largest same-setup deltas measured in the literature are non-algorithmic:
- retriever quality: 0.241 → 0.430 EM
- real vs stitched SFT trajectories: AIME24 avg@32 4.4 → 33.2
- test-time context management: +14 to +16 BrowseComp points
- retaining interleaved thinking: +12.6 BrowseComp points
- synthetic general-agent environments: needed for Tau2/MCP transfer

Credit-assignment algorithms show +5–15 pp, but only at ≤7–32B on short-horizon benchmarks.

### Cited Findings

#### Plain recipes that work (outcome reward, trajectory-level advantage)
- **Search-R1:** outcome-only EM reward ("we do not incorporate format rewards"), PPO or GRPO, ≤4 turns, 500 steps. Qwen2.5-7B-base average EM 0.431 (PPO). — [2503.09516 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516)
- **DeepSWE:** binary pass-all-tests reward, GRPO++ (all single-turn tricks plus compact filtering). Qwen3-32B: 23 → 42.2% Pass@1 in 200 steps on about 4.5K R2E-Gym problems, RL only with no SFT. — [DeepSWE model card [full]](https://huggingface.co/agentica-org/DeepSWE-Preview)
- **SA-SWE-32B:** LOO, no std, no KL or entropy, on-policy, 125 steps: 24.4 → 39.4%. — [2511.16108 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16108)
- **Tongyi DeepResearch (30B-A3B):** strict on-policy GRPO (ratio = 1), token-level loss, clip-higher, binary correctness reward, **no format reward** (SFT cold start already teaches format), length-truncated negatives excluded. BrowseComp 43.4, HLE 32.9, GAIA 70.9 (Avg@3). — [2510.24701 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24701)
- **Kimi-Researcher:** "primarily REINFORCE", strictly on-policy (format enforcers disabled), negative-sample control, γ-decay reward r·γ^{T−i}. HLE 8.6 → 26.9% "almost entirely through end-to-end RL". — [Kimi-Researcher blog [full]](https://moonshotai.github.io/Kimi-Researcher/)
- **GLM-4.5:** group-mean baseline over K traces, env tokens masked; a wrong tool-call format halts the rollout with zero reward. BrowseComp 26.4, SWE-bench Verified 64.2. **No before/after-RL numbers.** — [2508.06471 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06471)
- **SWE-RL:** single-turn, Agentless-style. Reward −1 for malformed output, otherwise difflib similarity to the oracle patch. GRPO, group 16, 1,600 steps, 16k context. Llama-3.3-70B: 41.0% SWE-bench Verified. Oracle-file repair: RL 34.8 vs SFT 29.6 vs base 5.4 greedy. Out-of-domain (base/SFT/RL): MATH strict 63.2/54.0/73.7, HumanEval+ 76.2/73.2/79.9, MMLU 86.49/85.26/86.82. No seeds. — [arXiv 2502.18449 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18449). Note that this is **not** a multi-turn agent. Its evidence is that RL beats SFT on the same seed data and transfers better.
- **SkyRL-v0:** about 300 training samples. 7B 11.0 → 14.6, 8B 3.6 → 9.4, 14B 18.0 → 21.6 on SWE-bench Verified. The algorithm details were in a Notion blog that did not render for me. — [Berkeley Sky page [full]](https://sky.cs.berkeley.edu/project/skyrl/)

This 0.19 spread exceeds every algorithmic delta in the Search-R1 line (masking 0.088; PPO vs GRPO ≤ 0.08).
- **Cold-start data:** real end-to-end tool trajectories vs stitched synthetic ones for SFT. Qwen3-4B-Instruct AIME24 avg@32 33.23 vs 4.38; AIME25 29.79 vs 3.65. — [DemyAgent, arXiv 2510.11701 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11701). WebSailor: RFT cold start (about 2k trajectories) then RL converges "significantly higher" than direct RL, with the widest gap on BrowseComp-en (figure only). — [2507.02592 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02592). WebDancer: RL without SFT on QwQ reaches only about 5% Pass@3 on GAIA. — [2505.22648 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22648)
- **Environment/task synthesis:** DeepSeek-V3.2 built 1,827 general-agent environments + 4,417 tasks, 24,667 code-agent, 50,275 search, and 5,908 code-interpreter tasks ("over 1,800 environments and 85,000 prompts"). Kimi K2: 3,000+ real MCP tools + 20,000+ synthetic tools, rubric-filtered simulated trajectories, real sandboxes for SWE. — [2507.20534 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534). Qwen3-Coder: "the key challenge of Agent RL lies in environment scaling", with 20,000 parallel environments. — [Qwen3-Coder blog [snippet]](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen3-coder/). GLM-5: over 10k verifiable SWE environments across 9 languages, plus thousands of terminal environments. — [2602.15763 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15763)
- **Test-time context management (no retraining):**
  - DeepSeek-V3.2 BrowseComp 51.4 → 67.6 with discard-all; summary-and-restart reaches 60.2 but uses 364 steps on average. — [2512.02556 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556)

  - GLM-5 55.3 → 62.0 with keep-recent-5 tool results → 75.9 hybrid (discard all history once context exceeds 32k). — [2602.15763 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15763)

#### Algorithmic deltas in the most controlled papers (for comparison)
- GiGPO vs GRPO: +13.9/+13.2 pp on ALFWorld (1.5B/7B), +10.6/+9.1 pp on WebShop, 3 seeds. — [2505.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10978)
- LOOP-token vs GRPO-no-KL on AppWorld Test-N: 71.3 vs 59.0 (best run); 3-run LOOP mean 66.4±4.8. — [2502.01600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01600)
- Clip-higher + overlong shaping (single-turn tricks) in an agentic TIR setting: GRPO-TCR matches GRPO-T's best AIME scores within 100 steps, about 25% of the compute; token-level beats sequence-level loss by about 3.9 pp at 4B and is "comparable" at 7B. — [2510.11701 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11701)

#### Reward design evidence
- **Format reward:** helps small base models: Qwen2.5-3B-base PPO 0.303 → 0.389, GRPO 0.312 → 0.382. 7B GRPO 0.350 → 0.420 but 7B PPO is flat (0.431 → 0.434), and 14B PPO drops (0.479 → 0.459). λ_f was grid-searched. — [2505.15117 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15117). Tongyi drops format reward after an SFT cold start ([2510.24701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24701)); WebSailor/WebDancer use 0.1·format + 0.9·answer ([2507.02592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02592), [2505.22648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22648)). **Grade B** (one grid-searched controlled study): useful for weak or base priors, unnecessary after SFT.
- **Intermediate retrieval reward:** hurts or is flat. 3B PPO 0.389 → 0.340; 7B PPO 0.434 → 0.419; 7B GRPO 0.420 → 0.410 (λ_r=0.1; larger λ_r is worse). — [2505.15117 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15117). R1-Searcher uses a stage-1 retrieval reward (0.5 if ≥1 retrieval) but does not ablate it. — [2503.05592 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05592). **Grade B against naive process rewards.**
- **Code-error penalty (−0.5 for unexecutable code):** no gain; the authors hypothesize it pushes the model toward overly simple code. — [ToRL 2503.23383 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23383)
- **SWE reward shape:** SWE-Gym with GRPO on Qwen3-8B: binary verified 0.042 vs ratio-of-tests 0.22 vs CodeRM-8B 0.072 vs GPT-4.1 judge 0.093, on **25 test instances**. — [2510.01132 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01132). This contrasts with DeepSWE and SA-SWE succeeding with binary reward at 32B. **Grade D**: dense partial credit helps when the pass rate is near zero, while binary works once the prior is strong enough.
- **LLM-judge process rewards** invite hacking: VAGEN's WM reward produced generic judge-pleasing text ([2510.16907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16907)). Kimi K2.5 uses multiple alternative rubrics in its generative reward models to reduce hacking ([2602.02276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02276)).
- **Efficiency rewards:**
  - Kimi-Researcher γ-decay r·γ^{T−i} ([blog](https://moonshotai.github.io/Kimi-Researcher/)).
  - MiniMax speed reward based on relative completion time, plus process reward for language mixing and tool errors, with reward-to-go advantage ([Forge](https://www.minimax.io/blog/forge-scalable-agent-rl-en-1779896141)).
  - Kimi K2 per-sample token budget with a truncation penalty ([2507.20534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534)).
  - K2.5 Toggle alternates budget-limited and unconstrained phases, cutting output length 25–30% with "negligible" loss, but warns of "length-overfitting" ([2602.02276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02276)).
  - None of these has an ablation table.

### Inferences
- Quantified ranking for search/web agents, from same-setup numbers: test-time context management (+14–16 BrowseComp) ≈ thinking retention (+12.6 BrowseComp, vendor-reported) > environment/retriever fidelity (+0.19 EM) ≈ cold-start data quality > env-token masking (+0.09 EM) > PPO-vs-GRPO choice (±0.08, sign varies) > format reward (+0.07 for small base models, ≈0 otherwise) > intermediate process rewards (≤0, harmful).
- For SWE agents, published open recipes reach about 40% Pass@1 at 32B with RL alone from Qwen3-32B (DeepSWE 42.2, SA-SWE 39.4), using trajectory-level LOO/GRPO. The frontier-lab gap (K2 65.8, GLM-4.5 64.2, V4 80.6) is not attributed to any credit-assignment algorithm in their reports. They attribute it to environment scale (10k+ environments), data synthesis, and model scale.
- "Algorithm" contributions that survive at scale are mostly about keeping updates on-policy-correct (masking, ratio control, truncation masking) and controlling length/latency incentives, not finer credit assignment.

### Gaps
- No frontier report publishes a before/after-RL agentic number together with an ablation of its RL algorithm choices. GLM-4.5 and GLM-5, DeepSeek-V3.2, and Kimi K2/K2.5 give no algorithm ablations for agent RL.
- No head-to-head study of trained-with-context-management vs test-time-only context management at scale. The Kimi-Researcher claim ("30% more iterations and higher performance") has no numbers; SUPO ([2510.06727 [snippet]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06727)) and Context-Folding ([2510.11967 [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11967), claiming about 10× smaller active context) were not read in full. ReSum (2509.13313) was not read.
- The MiniMax interleaved-thinking numbers come from a search summary of the vendor blog. I did not fetch the page.

## Q4. What do frontier labs report doing for agentic RL as of 2025–2026, and what does it imply about decisiveness? (Includes context management and asynchronous rollout.)

### Takeaway
As of October 2026, frontier labs converge on the same algorithmic core:
- a trajectory-level group baseline (GRPO/RLOO/REINFORCE; MiniMax adds reward-to-go)
- token-level losses with env tokens masked and token IDs preserved end to end
- importance-ratio masking/clipping against **rollout** log-probs to absorb train/infer mismatch and asynchrony
- staleness bounds or windowed scheduling
- dropping truncated or environment-broken trajectories
- explicit efficiency/length/latency terms

Their stated investments are environment scale (10k–100k+ environments and scaffolds), task synthesis, sandbox infrastructure, and context management. No lab reports GiGPO-style or critic-based turn-level credit assignment. Asynchronous rollout is treated as mandatory for long-horizon agents. It is justified by throughput (1.5–2.4× measured; 40× from prefix merging), and its algorithmic cost is the off-policy correction above.

### Cited Findings

#### Moonshot (Kimi)
- **K2 (July 2025):** K1.5 objective, a squared loss of (r − r̄ − τ·log π/π_old) over K samples, with the Muon optimizer; per-task token budget with truncation penalty; PTX auxiliary loss; temperature decay. Partial rollout pauses long-tail trajectories and resumes them next iteration. Environments run as services behind a Gym-like interface. Agentic SFT/RL data come from 3,000+ MCP tools and 20,000+ synthetic tools. τ²-Bench 66.1, ACEBench 76.5, SWE-bench Verified 65.8 single-attempt. **No RL ablations for agents.** — [2507.20534 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534)
- **Kimi-Researcher (June 2025):** REINFORCE, on-policy, negative-sample control, γ-decay. Context management keeps rollouts going past 50 iterations, versus overflow within 10 without it. Fully async rollout plus **turn-level partial rollout** (over-budget tasks go to a replay buffer and resume with updated weights) gives ≥1.5× rollout speedup. HLE 8.6 → 26.9%. — [blog [full]](https://moonshotai.github.io/Kimi-Researcher/)
- **K2.5 (Jan 2026):** token-level log-ratio clipping regardless of advantage sign (mismatch control). PARL trains the orchestrator only, with frozen sub-agents. Reward r = λ₁ r_parallel + λ₂ r_finish + r_perf, with λ annealed to 0 to counter "serial collapse" and "spurious parallelism". Toggle token-budget RL. Generative reward models with multiple rubrics. BrowseComp 60.6 → 74.9 (discard-all) → 78.4 (swarm). — [2602.02276 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02276)

#### Zhipu (GLM)
- **GLM-4.5 (Aug 2025):** group-mean baseline, env tokens masked, zero reward on tool-format error, iterative self-distillation (RL → SFT on RL outputs → harder RL), slime async disaggregated rollout, FP8 rollout with BF16 training. No agent-RL ablation numbers. — [2508.06471 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06471)
- **GLM-5 (Feb 2026):** asynchronous agent RL; token-level ratio against rollout log-probs with out-of-range tokens fully masked; staleness-based sample discard; optimizer reset after each inference-weight sync; TITO gateway; DP-aware consistent-hash routing for KV reuse; environment-collapse filtering with group padding. Over 10k SWE environments; o3-mini judge for search. Context management: keep-recent-5 (55.3 → 62.0 BrowseComp), hybrid 75.9. — [2602.15763 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15763) (evaluation section past 100k chars not read).

#### MiniMax
- **M1 (June 2025):** CISPO (2× fewer steps than DAPO on math), fp32 LM head, repetition truncation. SWE RL in containerized sandboxes with test-based reward. SWE-bench Verified 56.0 (80k). — [2506.13585 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13585)
- **M2 (Oct–Nov 2025):** interleaved thinking must be retained in context. MiniMax warns that third-party implementations that strip it "significantly reduce" performance. — [blog [snippet]](https://www.minimax.io/news/why-is-interleaved-thinking-important-for-m2)
- **M2.5 / Forge (Feb 2026):** CISPO with ratio clip(r, 0, 1+ε_high), stop-gradient. Â = Σ_{p≥t}(r^speed_p + r^perf_p) − B_i (reward-to-go). Process reward for language mixing and tool errors. **Context management is modeled as an agent action inside the environment dynamics.** Windowed-FIFO scheduler (window W ≈ 0.3N) bounds distribution drift. Prefix-tree merging gives a "40x training speedup" with exact loss equivalence. Trained across 100,000+ scaffolds/environments up to 200k context. **No ablation tables**; stability claimed qualitatively. — [Forge blog [full]](https://www.minimax.io/blog/forge-scalable-agent-rl-en-1779896141); [M2 series report 2605.26494 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26494) (training uses individual (s_t, a_t) pairs with episode-level credit; deliberately perturbed scaffolds for generalization).

#### DeepSeek
- **V3.2 (Dec 2025):** GRPO with unbiased KL, negative-advantage off-policy sequence masking, Keep Routing, Keep Sampling Mask. Prompt-only cold start for thinking-with-tools. 1,800+ synthesized environments / 85k prompts. Thinking retained across tool calls. Test-time discard-all context management takes BrowseComp 51.4 → 67.6. — [2512.02556 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556)
- **V4 (Apr 2026):** "The agent behavior was trained with RL against real tool environments". DSec Rust sandbox platform (function / container / Firecracker / QEMU; hundreds of thousands of concurrent sandboxes; preemption-safe trajectory replay without re-running tool calls). Reasoning is preserved across user turns when tools are present. SWE-bench Verified 80.6; Terminal-Bench 2.0 67.9. — [HF DeepSeek-V4 blog [full]](https://huggingface.co/blog/deepseekv4). Secondary sources conflict on whether the final post-training stage is RL or multi-teacher on-policy distillation from RL-trained specialists. The project's own V4 section ([`_sections/19-deepseek-v4.html`] in this repo) describes OPD as the merger, with RL used for the specialists. — [Kili analysis [snippet]](https://kili-technology.com/blog/data-story-deepseek-v4)

#### Asynchronous rollout: necessity and measured gains
- **Rationale:** in synchronous batches the step waits on the slowest trajectory. Long-tail agent trajectories span "dozens more tool calls and two orders of magnitude more tokens" (ASearcher, which trained runs with >100 tool calls / >400k tokens per the abstract, vs about 70 calls / 150k in Sec. 4.3, an internal inconsistency). ASearcher gives no throughput table. — [2508.07976 [full]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07976)
- **Measured:**
  - Kimi turn-level partial rollout ≥1.5× ([blog](https://moonshotai.github.io/Kimi-Researcher/)).
  - SkyRL-Agent async pipeline 1.55× over bounded async batching, about 90% GPU utilization ([2511.16108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16108)).
  - verl fully-async: streaming 1.6× and streaming + staleness 0.5 + partial rollout 2.35× (math RL, 7B). On Qwen3-30B-A3B, 1.72–2.01× vs colocated synchronous with **higher** final AIME acc (max 0.3813 vs 0.3500; single runs; 8k responses). Higher staleness gave "more obvious" gains ([verl fully_async.md](https://github.com/volcengine/verl/blob/main/docs/advance/fully_async.md), local clone dated 2026-05-22).
  - MiniMax prefix-tree merging 40× ([Forge](https://www.minimax.io/blog/forge-scalable-agent-rl-en-1779896141)).
- **Algorithmic consequence:** every async stack adds explicit off-policy control: GLM-5 staleness discard and ratio masking; MiniMax windowed FIFO and IS clip; verl `staleness_threshold`, approximating AReaL's decoupled PPO; DeepSeek negative-advantage masking; K2.5 token clipping. — sources as above.
- **Grade:** async rollout is **B for efficiency** (several independent throughput measurements). Its effect on the ceiling is unknown. No controlled agentic study holds total samples fixed and compares sync vs async final accuracy.

#### Context management during RL (training-time)
- Two designs exist. In the first, context management is part of environment dynamics and the policy is trained on per-step (s_t, a_t) pairs or segments: MiniMax ([2605.26494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26494)), SkyRL-Agent packing transitions that share a prefix ([2511.16108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16108)), SUPO's summarization-augmented policy gradient ([2510.06727 [snippet]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06727)), FoldGRPO with process rewards ([2510.11967 [abs]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11967)). In the second, context management is applied only at test time (DeepSeek-V3.2, K2.5, GLM-5 report test-time numbers only).
- Kimi-Researcher's only evidence for training with context management is "30% more iterations and higher performance" from an early ablation, with no numbers. — [blog](https://moonshotai.github.io/Kimi-Researcher/)
- **Grade:** test-time context management on BrowseComp is **A-** (three independent labs report +7 to +16 points with similar discard strategies). Training-time context management is **C**.

### Inferences
- What labs disclose matches the evidence grades. They keep what is grade-B-or-better or obviously required (masking, TITO, mismatch control, truncation masking, async with staleness bounds) and invest in environments. They skip grade-C credit-assignment methods. MiniMax's process reward plus reward-to-go is the main exception, and it is unablated.
- The genuinely new agent-specific algorithmic territory in 2026 is **multi-agent/latency-aware objectives**: PARL with annealed auxiliary rewards and the critical-steps metric; MiniMax's wall-clock speed reward. It is also **treating context operations as actions**. All of it is grade C today.
- Western frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) disclose no agentic RL algorithm details that I could find. Any statement about them would be speculation.
