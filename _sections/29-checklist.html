A researcher's checklist — what to verify before trusting an RL algorithm result

The infra survey's checklist asked what to know about a paper's system before trusting its numbers. This one asks what to know about its experiment. Each question corresponds to a failure documented somewhere in this survey. They are ordered by how often, in the 2025–2026 literature, the answer changes the conclusion.

  1. Which base models? If only Qwen2.5 or Qwen2.5-Math, the result is a hypothesis about that family. Random rewards improve Qwen2.5-Math by 21 points and hurt Llama and OLMo (spurious rewards).
  2. How many training seeds, and how many samples per evaluation problem? With one sample per AIME problem, two models must differ by about 25 points to be distinguishable; with 32 samples, about 5. Training-seed variance comes on top (three layers of noise).
  3. Was the baseline tuned as hard as the method, in the same code? A PPO baseline at 5 points and a DPO baseline that varies by 5.5 points between papers are both documented (weak baseline).
  4. How many optimizer steps per batch, how stale is the data, and where does πold come from? With one step and a recomputed πold, every clipped surrogate reduces to REINFORCE; a claimed loss improvement must then come from somewhere else (r ≡ 1).
  5. Is there a random- or format-reward control? Its gain is the part a sharpening update gets for free (pattern 3).
  6. Is the benchmark clean? Released after the model, or generated synthetically. Qwen2.5-Math reproduces MATH-500 problems from partial prompts (base models).
  7. Is the comparison matched in compute? In GPU-hours and rollouts, not optimizer steps. Dense-credit methods often spend more per step (compute accounting).
  8. Peak or final checkpoint? Best-in-window numbers hide late collapse, which is exactly what stability claims are about.
  9. Forward, cumulative or leave-one-out ablation? They answer different questions, and components that fix overlapping weaknesses look decisive in one and redundant in another (ScaleRL).
  10. Which ceiling — pass@1 or pass@k? RL usually raises the first and often lowers the second (what RL changes).
  11. Base or instruction-tuned starting point? Some recipe choices flip sign between them (base models).
  12. Does the implementation match the paper? Framework defaults differ from published methods in ways that change the mathematics (pitfalls).

A paper that does not answer the first three has not established its comparison. A paper that does not answer the first six cannot be placed in the evidence grades of this survey above C. The last six are where most of the disagreements between papers turn out to live.
