Twelve small experiments — how to do this yourself

The infra survey closed with a reading plan for doing the analysis yourself. The algorithmic equivalent is a set of experiments, because most of the open verdicts in this survey are open for lack of a controlled run, not for lack of ideas. Each experiment below is chosen to change the evidence grade of one verdict, and each fits on one node or a few with models of 1.5B to 8B parameters.

A shared protocol makes the results worth more than the sum of their parts: run on Qwen and on at least one other family (OLMo or Llama); use at least five training seeds; evaluate with many samples per problem on a benchmark released after the models; report compute in GPU-hours; include a random-reward control; and report final as well as best checkpoints.

#Question (verdict it would change)DesignWhat would decide it
1Does the r ≡ 1 identity hold in practice? (④)Remove mismatch (FP16 or batch-invariant kernels), one step per batch; compare GRPO, DAPO, GSPO, CISPO, SAPO with matched normalization; then repeat at 2, 4, 8 steps per batchDifferences within seed noise at one step, emerging as steps grow
2Do recipe choices transfer across families? (②③④)2×2×2 grid of std normalization, aggregation and clip-higher on Qwen base, Qwen instruct, OLMo and Llama, ≥ 1,000 stepsWhich sign flips track base-vs-aligned and which track family
3Is GRPO's easy-prompt up-weighting the harmful half? (②)Weights 1/√(p(1−p)), the same only for p < ½, and constant; on easy-heavy, mixed and hard-heavy dataAsymmetric weighting matches or beats both
4Can the asymptote be identified? (ceiling)Fit sigmoid and log-linear forms to the first half of long runs; test on the second half; bootstrap A and BIf A's interval is wider than 0.03, downgrade every "raises the ceiling" claim
5How do truncation handling and length budget interact? (⑦)Masking, soft penalty, interruption, nothing × budgets of 4k, 8k, 16k; log truncation rateHelps at short budgets, hurts at long ones; truncation rate as early warning
6Which mismatch corrections reduce bias? (⑥)Inject known mismatch (e.g. low-precision rollout); estimate a reference gradient from many samples; compare TIS, sequence masks, geometric filters, IcePop by cosine similarity at several lengthsA direct bias–variance measurement that does not exist yet
7Is staleness a staleness × LR budget? (⑥)Staleness 1–16 × three learning rates; record steps to collapseCollapse boundary along constant S·η
8Does clip-higher's effect scale with staleness? (④)εhigh ∈ {0.2, 0.28, 0.35} × 1 or 4 steps per batch, base and instruct; log clip rateEffect grows with clip rate, not with model type
9Is dense credit worth its rollouts? (PRM)Give GRPO the extra rollouts a tree or MC method uses (larger G or more steps); 4k and 16k contextGap closes under matched compute
10How much does preference data provenance outweigh the loss? (DPO)Loss (DPO, IPO, SimPO, LN-DPO) × data (offline, semi-online) × β grid, ≥ 3 seedsRatio of data effect to loss effect, with intervals
11Do states recur in open environments? (agentic)Count exact and near-duplicate states in SWE and web rolloutsIf near zero, state-grouped credit reduces to GRPO there
12Do framework KL defaults regularize? (⑤)KL as k1-loss, k2-loss, k3-loss, k1-in-reward and none, at low and high KL (e.g. on-policy distillation)k1-as-loss behaves like no KL; k3 diverges at high KL

Experiments 1, 4 and 2 would move the most verdicts at once: the first tests the identity behind axis ④, the second the identifiability behind every ceiling claim, the third the transfer behind most D grades. Each costs less than a typical paper proposing a new loss.
