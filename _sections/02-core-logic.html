Part I · Variational Foundations, Emergence & The Unified Spectrum

Deriving the exact target distribution of RL post-training, resolving the paradox of support sharpening vs. compositional emergence in parameter space, and unifying SFT, DPO, RLVR, PRMs, and On-Policy Distillation along two information-theoretic coordinates.

1. Variational Foundations: Support Conservation, Compositional Emergence & The Post-Training Spectrum

Every post-training algorithm for an autoregressive language model $\pi_\theta(y_{1:T} \mid x) = \prod_{t=1}^T \pi_\theta(y_t \mid x, y_{<t})$ is an attempt to solve a single variational problem under finite samples and finite compute. Understanding that variational problem—and where its tabular approximation breaks down in neural parameter space—immediately resolves the deepest debates in the post-training literature.

1.1 The Three-Layer Variational Reduction

Layer 1 (The Functional Objective). Given a prompt distribution $\mathcal{D}$, a black-box reward or verifier $R(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}$, and a reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, we seek a policy $\pi$ that maximizes expected reward subject to a reverse Kullback–Leibler divergence budget:

$$\max_{\pi}\; \mathcal{J}_{\beta}(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}}\!\left[\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)}\big[R(x, y)\big] - \beta\,D_{\text{KL}}\!\big(\pi(\cdot \mid x)\,\big\|\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot \mid x)\big)\right]$$

Why does RL differ fundamentally from supervised fine-tuning (SFT)? First, the sampling measure $\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x)$ depends on the parameters $\theta$: optimization moves the very distribution from which training data is drawn. Second, $R(x, y)$ is non-differentiable with respect to discrete tokens $y_{1:T}$. Differentiating $\mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta}[R(x, y)]$ therefore requires the score-function (log-derivative) identity $\nabla_\theta \pi_\theta(y\mid x) = \pi_\theta(y\mid x)\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y\mid x)$:

$$\nabla_\theta \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta}[R(x, y)] = \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x)}\!\left[\big(R(x, y) - b(x)\big)\sum_{t=1}^{|y|} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_t \mid x, y_{<t})\right]$$

where any prompt-only baseline $b(x)$ has zero expectation under $\pi_\theta$ because $\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi_\theta}[\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y\mid x)] = \nabla_\theta \sum_y \pi_\theta(y\mid x) = \nabla_\theta 1 = 0$.

Layer 2 (The Closed-Form Gibbs Target & Implicit Mirror-Descent Duality). Because $\mathcal{J}_\beta(\pi)$ is strictly concave in $\pi(\cdot \mid x)$ on the probability simplex, setting its functional Euler–Lagrange derivative $\frac{\delta \mathcal{J}_\beta}{\delta \pi(y\mid x)} = R(x,y) - \beta \log \frac{\pi(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} - \beta - \lambda(x) = 0$ yields the unique global optimizer:

$$\pi^*(y \mid x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)\,\exp\!\left(\frac{R(x, y)}{\beta}\right), \qquad Z(x) = \sum_{y'} \pi_{\text{ref}}(y' \mid x)\,\exp\!\left(\frac{R(x, y')}{\beta}\right)$$

This is exponential tilting (Gibbs reweighting) of the reference prior $\pi_{\text{ref}}$. Even when modern verifiable-reward RL (RLVR) sets the explicit KL penalty coefficient to $\beta = 0$ (Chapter 7.3), this equation still governs the trajectory of training. Why? Because policy gradient with step size $\eta$ performs entropic mirror descent starting from $\pi_0 = \pi_{\text{ref}}$:

$$\pi_{k+1}(y \mid x) \;\propto\; \pi_k(y \mid x)\,\exp\!\big(\eta\,\hat{A}_k(x, y)\big) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \pi_K(y \mid x) \;\propto\; \pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)\,\exp\!\left(\eta \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \hat{A}_k(x, y)\right)$$

Under stationary advantages, $K$ mirror-descent updates of step size $\eta$ trace the exact exponential-tilting family with an implicit temperature $\beta_{\text{eff}} \propto (\eta K)^{-1}$. Removing the explicit KL anchor simply lets $\beta_{\text{eff}} \to 0$ as $K$ grows—proving why an explicit KL penalty to $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ is mathematically dual to early stopping along the reward–KL Pareto frontier (Gao et al., 2210.10760). Three immediate laws follow from the Gibbs solution $\pi^*(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp(R/\beta)$:

  1. Function-space support conservation ($\operatorname{supp}(\pi^*) \subseteq \operatorname{supp}(\pi_{\text{ref}})$). Wherever $\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x) = 0$, multiplying by $\exp(R(x,y)/\beta)$ still yields strictly zero (`y9` in Figure 1 below). In non-parametric function space on a single prompt, RL reweights existing probability mass; it cannot create support ex nihilo.
  2. Mode sharpening as $\beta_{\text{eff}} \to 0$. As $\beta_{\text{eff}}$ shrinks, mass drains exponentially from suboptimal completions onto the highest-reward modes inside $\operatorname{supp}(\pi_{\text{ref}})$, raising single-sample accuracy ($\text{pass@1}$) while contracting distribution entropy and large-$k$ sample diversity.
  3. Exponential proxy amplification (Goodhart's Law). Whenever the reward function $R(x, y)$ assigns a false positive or inflated score to a degenerate sequence (`y7` in Figure 1) that also enjoys non-negligible prior mass $\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_7 \mid x)$, driving $\beta_{\text{eff}} \to 0$ concentrates $100\%$ of the asymptotic probability mass onto the hack. Reward hacking is not an optimization bug; it is the exact analytical optimum of $\mathcal{J}_\beta$ under an imperfect proxy $R$.

1.2 Law I · Resolving the Emergence Paradox: Function-Space Sharpening vs. Parameter-Space Compositional Transfer

A central controversy in the 2025–2026 literature pits two observations against each other. On one hand, controlled pass@$k$ studies show that RL post-training raises $\text{pass@1}$ while leaving large-$k$ coverage $\text{pass@}k$ ($k \in [128, 1024]$) flat or lower than the base model (Yue et al., 2504.13837; The Invisible Leash, 2507.14843). On the other hand, long-horizon RL runs (such as DeepSeek-R1-Zero or ProRL; 2505.24864) clearly solve complex multi-step reasoning problems where the base model's empirical pass@128 was zero. How can both be true?

First-principles analysis resolves this immediately by distinguishing sequence-level tabular tilting from token-level parameter-coupled dynamics:

  1. Why single-prompt tabular updates only sharpen: If $\pi(y \mid x)$ were parameterized as an independent lookup table for each full trajectory $y$ on prompt $x$, then any trajectory $y^*$ not sampled within the finite rollout budget $G$ receives zero positive gradient. Worse, because probability mass on the simplex sums to $1$, reinforcing the sampled correct trajectories drains mass from unsampled trajectories, causing $\text{pass@}k$ at large $k$ to contract toward the subset of modes hit early in training.
  2. How shared parameters $\theta$ create exponential compositional lift across prompts: An LLM is not a trajectory lookup table; it is a parameter-shared Markov transition kernel $\pi_\theta(y_t \mid s_t)$ where $s_t = (x, y_{<t})$. Under a gradient step $\Delta \theta = \eta \sum_{i, t} \hat{A}_{i,t} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t} \mid s_{i,t})$, the first-order change in log-probability for a trajectory $y'_{1:T'}$ on a different, harder prompt $x'$ is governed by the neural tangent (feature) kernel $\Theta_\theta(s, s') = \langle \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_t \mid s), \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y'_{t'} \mid s') \rangle$:
    $$\Delta \log \pi_\theta(y'_{1:T'} \mid x') = \eta \sum_{t'=1}^{T'} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \hat{A}_{i,t}\,\Theta_\theta\!\big((x', y'_{<t'}),\; (x_i, y_{i,<t})\big) + \mathcal{O}(\eta^2)$$
    Suppose solving a hard problem $x'$ requires chaining $m$ intermediate cognitive transitions (e.g., subgoal decomposition, self-verification, backtracking `"Wait, let me re-check..."`), each having a small prior transition probability $p_{\text{step}}$ under $\pi_{\text{ref}}$. Prior to RL, the joint probability of successfully chaining all $m$ verification steps is $P_{\text{ref}}(y^* \mid x') \propto p_{\text{step}}^m$—which for $p_{\text{step}} = 0.2$ and $m = 10$ is $10^{-7}$, completely invisible to $\text{pass@1024}$! However, if those same atomic verification transitions occur and succeed on easier prompts $x_i$ ($m = 1\text{ or }2$, where $p_{\text{step}} = 0.2$ is easily sampled within $G = 16$ rollouts), positive RL updates on $x_i$ increase the shared transition probability from $p_{\text{step}}$ to $p_{\text{step}} + \delta$. On the hard prompt $x'$, the trajectory probability is multiplied by:
    $$\frac{\pi_\theta(y^* \mid x')}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y^* \mid x')} \;\approx\; \left(1 + \frac{\delta}{p_{\text{step}}}\right)^m \;\approx\; \exp\!\left(m\,\frac{\delta}{p_{\text{step}}}\right)$$
    Lifting an atomic verification transition from $p_{\text{step}} = 0.20$ to $0.50$ across $m = 10$ compositional steps raises the full trajectory's probability by $(2.5)^{10} \approx 9{,}500\times$, moving it from $10^{-7}$ (unreachable) into the $10^{-3}$ regime where on-policy rollouts on $x'$ can finally sample and reinforce it directly!

Theorem 1 (Necessary & Sufficient Conditions for Capability Expansion vs. Pure Sharpening). Outcome-reward RL expands effective reasoning coverage beyond empirical $\text{pass@}k(\pi_{\text{ref}})$ if and only if three conditions hold simultaneously:

  1. Atomic motif support: The base model $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ already assigns non-trivial probability $p_{\text{step}} > 0$ to atomic verification, decomposition, and backtracking transitions on solvable bridge prompts (Gandhi et al., 2503.01307).
  2. Bridge curriculum density: The training set contains intermediate-difficulty prompts ($p_x \in (0.05, 0.80)$) where those atomic transitions correlate with higher binary reward $R(x, y)$.
  3. Entropy survival: Token-level policy entropy $\mathcal{H}(\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid s_t))$ is prevented from collapsing prematurely onto superficial formatting or majority-vote modes before cross-prompt kernel transfer $\Theta_\theta$ amplifies the multi-step compositions.

When Condition 1 or 3 fails, RL degenerates into pure distribution sharpening, bounded above by the base model's majority-vote accuracy:

$$\text{pass@1}_{\text{pure-sharpening}}(\pi_\theta) \;\le\; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}}\!\Big[\mathbf{1}\big\{\arg\max_y \pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x) \in \mathcal{Y}^*(x)\big\}\Big] = \operatorname{maj@\infty}(\pi_{\text{ref}})$$

How Theorem 1 denoises the "Sharpening vs. Expansion" and "Base Model Ceiling" literature:

  • Why pass@$k$ shrinks on static single-difficulty runs: On Omni-MATH (Yue et al., 2504.13837), standard GRPO raised pass@1 from $9.9\%$ to $42.5\%$ while pass@256 fell from $67.2\%$ to $64.3\%$, and on AIME-24 at $k=1{,}024$ the RL-solved set was a strict subset of the base model's. Similarly, tracking individual completions showed $163$ solutions leaving support versus $36$ entering (2507.14843). In contrast, when ProRL trained for $2{,}000+$ steps with explicit KL/optimizer resets and curriculum progression, Reasoning Gym accuracy rose from $4.24\%$ to $59.06\%$, unlocking multi-step compositions that were empirically $0/128$ under $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ (2505.24864; 2610.04158).
  • Why priming atomic behaviors before RL dwarfs changing the RL loss: Llama-3.2-3B stalls under RL on Countdown because its pre-RL support lacks verification and backtracking motifs (Condition 1 fails). Fine-tuning Llama-3.2-3B on a small set of trajectories containing verification and backtracking motifs—even when those priming trajectories end in incorrect final answers—causes subsequent RL to match Qwen-2.5-3B's steep trajectory (Gandhi et al., 2503.01307) B. Likewise, continuing pre-training on 220B math-rich tokens (OctoThinker, 2506.20512) closes the cross-family RL gap without touching the RL algorithm.
Case Study 1 · The Pure-Sharpening Bound in Action

Why random rewards add +21 points on Qwen2.5-Math and hurt Llama and OLMo

Theorem 1's pure-sharpening bound ($\text{pass@1} \to \operatorname{maj@\infty}(\pi_{\text{ref}})$) immediately explains one of the most striking anomalies in the RLVR literature. Shao et al. (Spurious Rewards, 2506.10947) trained Qwen2.5-Math-7B on MATH-500 using deliberately corrupted or uninformative rewards:

Why does a zero-information reward produce a $+21.4$-point jump on Qwen2.5-Math-7B? Because Qwen2.5-Math-7B has an unusually large gap between its initial mean accuracy ($\text{pass@1}$) and its modal strategy accuracy ($\operatorname{maj@\infty}$), driven by two factors A:

  1. Pre-existing high-accuracy modal strategy: Prior to RL, $65\%$ of Qwen2.5-Math-7B rollouts use Python code-reasoning ($60.9\%$ accuracy) while $35\%$ use prose ($28.0\%$ accuracy). Any update that sharpens the policy toward its dominant mode pushes Python usage to $95.6\%$ (2506.10947), harvesting $+20+$ points without learning a single new mathematical fact. Even changing the prompt wrapper alone swings Qwen2.5-Math's pre-RL benchmark average from $0.0\%$ (R1 template) to $38.2\%$ (no template; Dr. GRPO, 2503.20783).
  2. Pre-training benchmark contamination: Given the first $60\%$ of a MATH-500 problem, Qwen2.5-Math-7B completions reproduce the exact benchmark text $54.6\%$ of the time versus $3.8\%$ for Llama-3.1-8B (2507.10532). On post-cutoff synthetic benchmarks—or on decontaminated models like OLMo 3 (2512.13961) and Llama—random rewards yield $\le 0$ gain while ground-truth verifiers continue to work (2512.16912).

First-principles takeaway: Any gain within the $\operatorname{maj@}k(\pi_{\text{ref}}) - \text{pass@1}(\pi_{\text{ref}})$ gap can be achieved by blind sharpening (including one-shot RLVR, 2504.20571, and label-free entropy minimization, 2505.15134). An RL algorithm only proves it extracts information from the reward to the extent that it beats a random-reward / format-only control on an uncontaminated base model.

1.3 Law II · The Unified Post-Training Spectrum (From SFT & DPO to RLVR & OPD)

Why has the post-training field fractured into SFT, Rejection Sampling (RFT/ReST), Offline Preference Optimization (DPO/IPO/SimPO), Online Preference Optimization, Outcome-Reward RL (RLVR/GRPO), Process Reward Models (PRMs), and On-Policy Distillation (OPD)? From first principles, every paradigm is uniquely specified by **three mathematical coordinates** in how it approximates $\pi^*$:

  1. Rollout Measure ($\mu$): Are training trajectories drawn from a static external distribution $\mu_{\text{off}}(y\mid x)$ (teacher or frozen dataset) or from the student's own live autoregressive distribution $\pi_\theta(y\mid x)$?
  2. Supervision Channel Capacity ($\mathcal{C}$, in bits per token): Does the supervision signal provide $\mathcal{O}(\log|\mathcal{V}|) \approx 10\text{ bits}$ at every token $t$, or a single binary scalar $R(x, y) \in \{0, 1\}$ with Shannon mutual information $I(R; Y\mid x) = H_{\text{Ber}}(p_x) \le 1\text{ bit}$ smeared across $T = 10^4$ tokens ($\le 10^{-4}\text{ bits/token}$)?
  3. Gradient Sign ($\text{sgn}(\hat{A})$): Does the update only push probability mass up on target tokens ($\hat{A} > 0$, mode-covering forward KL), or does it also apply negative gradients ($\hat{A} < 0$, mode-seeking reverse KL) to actively prune the student's false modes?

SFT injects initial atomic motifs ($\operatorname{supp}(\pi_{\text{ref}})$); Outcome RLVR (GRPO/RLOO) is the only paradigm that can push beyond human/teacher demonstrations using a $1$-bit verifier, making it the engine for training domain specialists; and On-Policy Distillation (OPD) provides $10^4\times$ higher supervision bandwidth per token on the student's own rollouts, making it the mathematically optimal way to merge multiple RLVR specialists into a single generalist model (as in DeepSeek-V3.2, DeepSeek-V4, Qwen3, and GLM-4.5).
