Preface: Navigating 10,000 Post-Training Papers via the Two-Layer Fixed-Point Coordinate System ($\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B} \times \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{D}$)

Between classical policy gradients (REINFORCE, PPO), offline and online preference optimization (DPO, IPO, SimPO, KTO, ORPO, Online DPO), critic-free group reinforcement learning (RLOO, GRPO, Dr. GRPO, DAPO, MaxRL), off-policy and Mixture-of-Experts stabilizers (CISPO, GSPO, GMPO, SAPO, TOPR, IcePop, Routing Replay R3), step-level process rewards (PRMs, VinePPO, PRIME, PURE), multi-turn agentic RL (Search-R1, GiGPO), self-play games (SPPO, Nash-MD), and On-Policy Distillation (GKD, MiniLLM, OPD, DeepSeek-V4), the LLM post-training literature has grown to more than 10,000 papers.

When indexing what a paper modifies in a training codebase, it is natural to list five operational modules: Objective (what distribution or equilibrium is targeted), Signal (where rewards come from), Data (how rollouts are sampled and filtered), Estimator (how rewards turn into gradients), and Update Control (clipping, importance ratios, and steps per batch). While a flat five-module list is useful for indexing which line of code a paper touched, treating those five dimensions as parallel peers throws away the single most important causal structure in reinforcement learning: which choices determine where the policy converges (the stationary fixed point $\pi^*$), and which choices only determine how it gets there (bias, variance, velocity, and stability)?

1. The Fundamental Split: Target Layer ($\pi^*$) vs. Approximation Layer

Return to the closed-form solution of KL-regularized reinforcement learning (Chapter 1.1):

$$\pi^*(y\mid x) \;\propto\; \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\,\exp\!\Big(\frac{R(x, y)}{\beta}\Big)$$
  • The Target Layer ($\mathbf{A}$: Determines where the fixed point or optimal set $\pi^*$ is): Comprises the supervision signal and its error structure (A1), the prompt-level utility weighting $F(p_x)$ (A2), the response-length weighting $c_{i,t}$ (A3), and the regularizer $\beta\,\mathrm{KL}$ (A4)—acting on top of the given Base Model $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ (which fixes the initial support $\operatorname{supp}(\pi_{\text{ref}})$). Layer $\mathbf{A}$ determines the asymptotic ceiling $A$ and dictates how the objective can be reward-hacked.
  • The Operating Regime ($\mathbf{B}$) & Approximation Layer ($\mathbf{C}$: Determines whether and how fast we reach $\pi^*$—and selects among modes when $\beta=0$): Comprises the off-policy regime ($\mu$ steps/batch, async lag $\tau$, hybrid-engine/MoE drift $\delta_{\text{sys}}$ in B1), the 4-quadrant importance-ratio gate $\psi(r, \hat{A})$ (C1, which is algebraically empty $\varnothing$ when B1 is strictly on-policy $r\equiv 1$), the credit/baseline estimator (C2), and the effective KL step size (C3). Note a crucial mathematical subtlety: when $\beta > 0$, $\pi^*$ is a unique interior distribution determined solely by Layer $\mathbf{A}$; when $\beta = 0$ and $R(x,y)\in\{0,1\}$ (standard modern RLVR), $\pi^*$ degenerates into the entire simplex face $\Delta(\mathcal{Y}^+(x))$ of distributions supported on correct responses ($p_x = 1$), so Layer $\mathbf{C}$'s implicit mirror-descent trajectory from $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ selects which mode on that optimal simplex survives.
  • The Dynamical Phase Boundary ($\mathbf{D}$: Entropy Drift & Support Reachability): Because online RL samples from $\pi_k$ itself, Layer $\mathbf{C}$ exhibits a piecewise regime: inside the stable dynamical interior, Layer $\mathbf{C}$ affects only variance and convergence velocity $B$; however, if Layer $\mathbf{C}$ crosses a dynamical collapse threshold (e.g., Q-IV negative-logit explosion on shared prefixes, un-replayed MoE routing drift, or length penalties under $L_{\text{cap}} < T^*(x)$), it triggers irreversible entropy/support collapse ($\mathcal{H}(\pi_k) \to 0$, D1), directly lowering the realized ceiling $A$.
The Master Fixed-Point Diagnostic. Whenever a paper proposes a new post-training modification, the decisive question is not "Which module did it edit?" but:
"Did this modification alter the Target Layer $\mathbf{A}$ ($\pi^*$ or cross-prompt weighting $F(p_x)$), or did it only alter the Approximation Layer $\mathbf{C}$?"
  • If Layer A is unchanged: It is an efficiency, variance, or off-policy stability intervention. Inside the stable dynamical interior, its gain appears in convergence speed $B$ or high-$\mu$ throughput—unless it prevents a phase-boundary collapse in D1.
  • If Layer A is changed: It is a Target-Layer modification—very often disguised as an estimator or update-control trick (Section 2.3):
    • GRPO dividing by group std $\sigma_G$ looks like estimator variance normalization, but actually changes A2 from $\sum_x p_x$ to $\sum_x 2\arcsin\sqrt{p_x}$ (re-allocating cross-prompt gradient flow toward $p_x \to 1$ and $p_x \to 0$ extremes).
    • Per-sequence $\frac{1}{|y_i|}$ vs. token-mean aggregation looks like an estimator reduction detail, but actually changes A3 by reweighting trajectories by length $|y_i|^{-1}$.
    • Putting the $k_3$ KL estimator directly inside the differentiated loss looks like a regularizer implementation detail, but has a biased gradient that shifts A4 away from the reverse-KL fixed point $\pi^*$.
    • Dynamic sampling (dropping $p_x \in \{0, 1\}$ groups) looks like a data distribution change, but leaves the target direction unchanged because $p_x \in \{0, 1\}$ groups had zero expected gradient anyway!

2. A Two-Layer Coordinate System with Two Falsifiable Predictive Rules

By reading coordinates directly from the canonical update equation (Section 2.3)

$$g(\theta) = \sum_x \underbrace{w(p_x)}_{\text{A2}} \sum_{i,t} \underbrace{c_{i,t}}_{\text{A3}}\,\underbrace{\psi(r_{i,t}, \hat{A}_{i,t})}_{\text{C1, C2 (iff B1 }\ne\text{ on-policy)}}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}) - \underbrace{\beta\,\nabla_\theta\widehat{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta, \pi_{\text{ref}})}_{\text{A4}}$$

and merging sub-variants whose controlled differences fall inside the evaluation Minimum Detectable Effect ($\text{MDE} \approx \pm 4.6$ points on single-seed AIME30 at $k=32$), our coordinate table yields two piecewise, falsifiable predictions (audited across 105 primary papers in Section 2.3):

  1. Rule 1 (Same Target $\mathbf{A} \implies$ Same Ceiling $A$): Under matched boundary conditions Cond ($\pi_{\text{ref}}$, prompt difficulty distribution, and compute budget) and absence of early entropy collapse (D1), any two algorithms with the same Target Layer coordinates (A1, A2, A3, A4) reach the same asymptotic ceiling within the noise floor (e.g., 3-seed `VC-PPO` $50.9$ vs. `GRPO` $50.0$, and single-run `GRPO` $50.2$, `RLOO` $49.9$, `VC-PPO` $50.4$ in GenAC; single-run `Semi-Online DPO` $58.9$ and `Online DPO` $58.7$ vs. `GRPO` $58.1$ on MATH-500 in 2506.21495).
  2. Rule 2 (Same $(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}) \implies$ Same Training Curve): When operating regime B1 and approximation coordinates (C1, C2, C3) also match (with effective KL step size C3 tuned to the same scale), the entire training trajectory coincides within noise (e.g., `DPO`, `IPO`, `SimPO`, and `ORPO` within $\sim 1.5$ AlpacaEval LC points once $\beta$ and LR are swept in 2502.01237; on-policy token-level PG and off-policy token-level clipping with routing replay `R2/R3` stabilizing Qwen3-30B-A3B without sequence-level `GSPO` in 2512.01374, and `GSPO`, `TIS`, `IcePop`, `FP16`, and `CIS` landing within $\sim 1.6$ points across 3 MoE models $\times$ 3 seeds in 2609.32444).
