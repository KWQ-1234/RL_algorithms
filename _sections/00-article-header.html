RL Algorithms for the Mathematically Inclined

Which changes to LLM post-training actually matter, and how to tell

Abstract. Since 2024, reinforcement learning for language models has produced a long list of named algorithms, each reporting gains. This survey reads them as settings of one policy-gradient estimator and judges each change along eight design axes, three algorithm families and five evidence case studies. Every verdict is stated for four quantities — the ceiling training reaches, the compute it takes, the stability of long runs, and side effects such as response length — and graded by the strength of its controlled evidence. It is a companion to RL Infrastructure for the Mathematically Inclined, which asks the same question of the systems underneath.

About 130 minutes to read. Around 105 papers cited, with evidence current as of October 2026.
