11. Twelve Decisive Experiments, Open Questions & Reading List

Most of the C and D verdicts in this survey remain open not for lack of algorithmic ideas, but for lack of controlled, multi-seed experiments that isolate a single variable. This final chapter lays out twelve decisive experiments that fit on 1–4 nodes (1.5B–8B models), eight open mathematical questions at the algorithm–theory boundary, and a curated reading list.

11.1 Twelve Decisive Experiments to Run Yourself

A shared experimental protocol makes these runs worth far more than the sum of their parts: run on both a Qwen model and at least one non-Qwen family (OLMo 3 or Llama 3.1); use $\ge 5$ training seeds; evaluate with $K \ge 32$ rollouts per problem on a contamination-checked benchmark; report compute in GPU-hours; include a random-reward or format-only control; and report both final and peak checkpoints.

11.2 Eight Open Mathematical Questions

Each question below marks a point where a rigorous theorem or tight statistical bound would settle an empirical debate that current papers can only grade heuristically:

  1. Identifiability of the compute asymptote $A$: Sigmoid and unbounded log-linear curves fit finite RL training trajectories nearly equally well (arXiv:2610.04158). Under what conditions on the prompt difficulty distribution is the asymptote $A$ statistically identifiable from compute budget $C$, and how wide is its minimax confidence interval?
  2. Tight bias bounds for length-normalized importance filtering: Token-level truncated IS corrects per-step conditionals without correcting prefix state visitation, incurring bias that grows polynomially in sequence length $|y|$ (Chapter 4.2). Length-normalized geometric filters ($\text{GSPO}, \text{GMPO}$) stabilize long-horizon training empirically without a matching bias-variance bound. What is the exact bias of geometric-mean ratio clipping as a function of $|y|$ and per-token Rényi divergence?
  3. First-principles derivation of the staleness $\times$ step-size stability law: Empirically, $\eta_{\max} \propto (\mu + \tau)^{-1}$ (Chapter 5.2). Proving a tight lower bound showing that mirror descent with stale gradients diverges whenever $(\mu + \tau)\eta$ exceeds a curvature-dependent threshold would turn a tuning heuristic into a formal system-design law.
  4. Optimal prompt-weighting transform $F(p)$ across training time: Every advantage normalization ascends $\sum_x F(p_x)$ (Law 2). Given an initial pass-rate distribution $\rho_0(p)$ across prompts, what schedule of transforms $F_t(p)$ maximizes final pass@1—or pass@$k$—under a fixed rollout budget?
  5. Cross-prompt generalization and support expansion: On a single prompt, outcome-reward updates only reweight existing support (Layer 1). New capabilities must arise from cross-prompt parameter sharing during gradient updates. Can we bound how much probability mass leaks onto novel reasoning chains on prompt $x'$ when updating on prompt $x$ via neural tangent or feature-learning kernels?
  6. Tight upper bounds on label-free sharpening: Updates that concentrate mass push pass@1 toward $\text{maj@}\infty(\pi_{\text{ref}})$ (Law 3). How closely do entropy minimization and asymmetric clipped random-reward updates approach this theoretical limit, and at what rate?
  7. Safe intermediate credit between potential shaping and free learned scores: Potential-based shaping $\phi_t = \Phi(s_{t+1}) - \Phi(s_t)$ preserves $\pi^*$ exactly, while unconstrained additive PRMs are exploited (Law 5). Which bounded or min-aggregated step-reward operators guarantee $\varepsilon$-preservation of the ground-truth optimal policy set?
  8. Multi-step policy entropy dynamics under clipping and batch reuse: The single-step entropy change equals $-\eta\,\text{Cov}_{\pi_\theta}(\log\pi_\theta, \hat{A})$ (Chapter 7.3). Can multi-step entropy collapse under $\mu > 1$ clipped updates be predicted analytically from initial rollout log-probability variance?

11.3 Curated Reading List

Twenty-two foundational and recent (through 2026) papers, selected for how sharply they clarify the mathematical and experimental picture rather than citation volume. Read the controlled measurement studies first; they provide the lens needed to interpret the rest.

1. Controlled Studies & Measurement Hygiene
  • The Art of Scaling Reinforcement Learning Compute for LLMs (ScaleRL) — separates asymptote $A$ from efficiency $B$ across 400k GPU-hours; read the leave-one-out appendices alongside the main text.
  • A Sober Look at Progress in Language Model Reasoning (Hochlehnert et al.) — quantifies evaluation variance on AIME/AMC and shows single-seed claims sit inside noise.
  • Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective (Dr. GRPO) — identifies the $1/|y_i|$ length bias and $\div\;\text{std}_G$ prompt-difficulty bias.
  • Tricks or Traps? A Deep Dive into RL for LLM Reasoning — demonstrates how algorithm rankings flip between base and instruction-tuned checkpoints.
  • GenAC (2026) — compares GRPO, RLOO, and critic-based PPO with one variable changed at a time, showing all three converge within $\sim 1$ point.
  • Implementation Matters in Deep Policy Gradients (Engstrom et al., 2020) — the classic deep-RL precursor proving code-level choices dominate named surrogate losses.
2. First-Principles Mechanisms & Theory
  • Does RL Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity Beyond the Base Model? (Yue et al.) — pass@$k$ convergence analysis showing RL sharpens within base-model support while distillation expands it.
  • Prompt Weighting as an Implicit Objective (2026) & MaxRL (2026) — unify advantage normalization rules via $F'(p) = w(p)$.
  • The Entropy Mechanism of RL for Reasoning LMs (Cui et al.) — derives the exact one-step entropy-covariance identity.
  • On a Few Pitfalls in KL Divergence Gradient Estimation for RL — proves $k_1$-as-loss has zero expected gradient and $k_3$-as-loss estimates the forward-KL gradient.
  • Tapered Off-Policy REINFORCE (TOPR) — explains why Quadrant III ($\hat{A} < 0$) causes unbounded gradient variance off-policy.
  • Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization (Gao et al.) — establishes the functional form of Goodhart's Law against learned reward proxies.
3. Off-Policy Staleness, Engine Mismatch & Numerics
  • Your Efficient RL Framework Secretly Brings You Off-Policy RL Training (Yao et al.) — documents Gap 1 between vLLM/SGLang rollout engines and FSDP/Megatron trainers.
  • When Speed Kills Stability (Liu et al.) & VeXact (2026) — token vs. sequence mismatch corrections and bitwise-exact rollout verification.
  • Stabilizing RL for LLMs (Qwen Team) & CIS (2026) — controlled multi-seed evaluations of importance-sampling corrections and MoE stability.
  • Rollout Routing Replay (R3) — solves MoE expert-routing discontinuity between inference and multi-epoch training.
  • AReaL & Staleness-Learning-Rate Scaling (2026) — decoupled proximal policies and the $(\mu+\tau)\eta$ stability budget.
4. Base-Model Priors & Neighbouring Families
  • Spurious Rewards: Rethinking Training Signals in RLVR & The Mirage of Spurious Correlations — read as a pair on Qwen2.5-Math contamination and sharpening vs. true learning.
  • Cognitive Behaviors that Enable Self-Improving Reasoners (Gandhi et al.) — shows base-model verification primitives govern downstream RL ceiling.
  • Unpacking DPO and PPO, Generalized Preference Optimization & Bridging Offline and Online RL — prove on-policy pair generation dominates loss-function algebra.
  • PURE — dissects Process Reward Model (PRM) hacking and bounded credit assignment.
  • Search-R1 — establishes state-token masking and raw token-ID preservation for multi-turn agentic RL.
