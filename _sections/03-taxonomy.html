2. Problem-Driven Algorithm Evolution, the Canonical Estimator & the Two-Layer Sufficiency Coordinate System ($\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B} \times \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{D}$)

In this chapter, we first derive the core algorithms of LLM reinforcement learning from a problem-solving perspective—showing the exact mathematical bottleneck each algorithm solved and the new bottleneck it created (Section 2.1). We then factorize their equations into a single canonical estimator and prove the $r \equiv 1$ On-Policy Collapse Theorem (Section 2.2). Finally, we organize the entire post-training design space into the Two-Layer Sufficiency Coordinate System ($\text{Target }\mathbf{A} \times \text{Regime }\mathbf{B} \times \text{Approximation }\mathbf{C} \to \text{Dynamics }\mathbf{D}$), dissect off-policy and credit choices via $2\times 2$ Quadrant Maps, and verify the two predictive rules against controlled same-cell and cross-cell experiments across 105 papers (Section 2.3).

2.1 From Problems to Algorithms: The Five-Stage Constructive Evolution

Stage 1 · REINFORCE (1992): Solving Non-Differentiable Discrete Rewards

The Problem: We want to maximize $\mathcal{J}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}[R(x, y)]$, where $y = (y_1, \dots, y_T)$ is a discrete token sequence sampled from an autoregressive transformer and $R(x, y)$ is a non-differentiable black-box verifier or reward model.

The Algorithmic Solution: Apply the score-function identity $\nabla_\theta \pi_\theta(y\mid x) = \pi_\theta(y\mid x)\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y\mid x)$ and factorize $\log \pi_\theta(y\mid x) = \sum_{t=1}^{|y|} \log \pi_\theta(y_t\mid x, y_{<t})$:

$$\hat{g}_{\text{REINFORCE}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G} R(x, y_i) \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t} \mid x, y_{i,<t}), \qquad y_i \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid x)$$

What Breaks in Practice: (1) Sign ambiguity & $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ variance: if $R(x, y) \in [0, 1]$, bad trajectories get zero gradient instead of negative suppression, and summing uncentered score functions across $T = 10{,}000$ tokens causes variance to explode as $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$. (2) Single-step sample waste ($\mu = 1$): because the expectation is under $\pi_\theta$, taking a single optimizer step invalidates the batch $\{y_i\}$, wasting $90\%+$ of GPU time on autoregressive generation.

Stage 2 · PPO-RLHF (2017 / InstructGPT 2022): Solving Sample Reuse ($\mu > 1$) & Token Credit

The Problem: How can we reuse a generated batch of rollouts $\{y_i\} \sim \pi_{\text{old}}$ for $\mu > 1$ gradient steps without catastrophic off-policy divergence, while localizing credit across $T$ tokens?

The Algorithmic Solution: PPO introduces (a) a learned token-level value critic $V_\phi(s_t)$ with Generalized Advantage Estimation $\hat{A}_{i,t}^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}$, and (b) the per-token importance ratio $r_{i,t}(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x, y_{i,<t})}{\pi_{\text{old}}(y_{i,t}\mid x, y_{i,<t})}$ inside a pessimistic clipped surrogate:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{PPO}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_{i,t}\!\Big[\min\!\big(r_{i,t}(\theta)\hat{A}_{i,t}^{\text{GAE}},\; \operatorname{clip}(r_{i,t}(\theta), 1-\varepsilon, 1+\varepsilon)\hat{A}_{i,t}^{\text{GAE}}\big)\Big], \qquad r_{i,t}(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid s_{i,t})}{\pi_{\text{old}}(y_{i,t}\mid s_{i,t})}$$

What Breaks at Reasoning Scale: (1) The 4-model memory wall: maintaining Actor $\pi_\theta$, Critic $V_\phi$, Reference $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, and Reward Model $R_{\text{RM}}$ simultaneously doubles memory and adds $1.6\times$ compute overhead per step. (2) Long-CoT GAE attenuation: when a binary outcome reward $R \in \{0, 1\}$ arrives only at terminal token $T = 8{,}000$, standard $\lambda = 0.95$ GAE attenuates the reward signal at token $t$ by $\lambda^{T-t}$ ($0.95^{200} \approx 3\times 10^{-5}$), leaving the first $7{,}800$ tokens blind to whether the final answer was right or wrong (Chapter 4.1).

Stage 3 · RLOO & GRPO (2024 / DeepSeekMath & DeepSeek-R1): Eliminating the Value Critic

The Problem: In verifiable reasoning (math, code, logic), we only care about sequence-level outcome reward $R(x, y_i) \in \{0, 1\}$. Can we eliminate the learned critic $V_\phi$ entirely by estimating the baseline $b(x)$ directly from a group of $G$ parallel rollouts $\{y_1, \dots, y_G\} \sim \pi_{\text{old}}(\cdot\mid x)$ on the same prompt?

The Algorithmic Solutions:

  • RLOO (Leave-One-Out REINFORCE; Ahmadian et al., 2402.14740): Subtracts the average reward of the other $G-1$ rollouts on prompt $x$, yielding an exact unbiased sequence advantage: $$\hat{A}_i^{\text{RLOO}} = R_i - \frac{1}{G - 1}\sum_{j \ne i} R_j = \frac{G}{G - 1}\big(R_i - \bar{R}_G\big), \qquad \text{where } \bar{R}_G = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{j=1}^G R_j$$
  • GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization; Shao et al., 2402.03300; DeepSeek-R1, 2501.12948): Embeds the group baseline inside PPO's clipped objective for $\mu > 1$ multi-epoch updates, but adds three extra design choices: (1) divides the centered reward by the group standard deviation $\sigma_G = \operatorname{std}(\{R_1,\dots,R_G\})$, (2) averages the token loss within each response by $\frac{1}{|y_i|}$, and (3) adds a per-token $k_3$ KL penalty directly to the loss:
    $$\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G} \frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \Big[\min\!\big(r_{i,t}\hat{A}_i^{\text{GRPO}},\, \operatorname{clip}(r_{i,t}, 1-\varepsilon, 1+\varepsilon)\hat{A}_i^{\text{GRPO}}\big) - \beta\,k_3(\pi_\theta, \pi_{\text{ref}})\Big], \qquad \hat{A}_i^{\text{GRPO}} = \frac{R_i - \bar{R}_G}{\sigma_G + \epsilon}$$

What Breaks When Scaling GRPO to Long-CoT Reasoning: Scaling GRPO to $16\text{k–}64\text{k}$ reasoning traces at $\mu = 4\text{–}16$ updates per batch exposed four mathematical pathologies inside $\mathcal{J}_{\text{GRPO}}$:

  1. Pathology 1 (Objective & Length Distortion $\to$ Target Layer A2, A3): Dividing by $\sigma_G = \sqrt{p_x(1-p_x)}$ gives a $3\times$ gradient boost not only to hard prompts ($p_x \to 0$) but also to trivial, nearly solved prompts ($p_x \to 1$)! Simultaneously, dividing each trajectory by its own length $\frac{1}{|y_i|}$ penalizes long wrong answers ($R_i = 0 \implies \hat{A}_i < 0$) less per token than short wrong answers, causing incorrect chains of thought to bloat toward $L_{\max}$.
  2. Pathology 2 (Rare-Token Clipping Starvation & Entropy Collapse $\to$ Approximation C1 & Dynamics D1): Under symmetric $\varepsilon = 0.20$, a rare reasoning pivot token (`"Wait"`, `"Alternatively"`) with $\pi_{\text{old}}(y_{i,t}) = 10^{-3}$ hits the upper clip wall $r_{i,t} = 1.20$ as soon as its probability rises by $0.0002$ on micro-step 1, getting zero-masked for the remaining $\mu - 1$ steps—while high-probability mode tokens ($\pi_{\text{old}} = 0.50$) can rise by $0.10$, collapsing policy entropy $\mathcal{H}(\pi_\theta) \to 0$.
  3. Pathology 3 (Zero-Variance Dead Groups $\to$ Dynamics D2): Whenever all $G$ rollouts on prompt $x$ fail ($\sum R_i = 0$) or succeed ($\sum R_i = G$), $\hat{A}_i^{\text{GRPO}} \equiv 0$. These $0$-bit groups contribute zero gradient signal while shrinking the effective batch size.
  4. Pathology 4 (Negative-Quadrant & Hybrid-Engine Explosion $\to$ Regime B1 & Ratio Control C1): Over $T = 16{,}000$ tokens at $\mu = 4\text{–}16$ steps/batch—especially on Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models where vLLM inference and Megatron training route tokens to different experts—negative-advantage tokens ($\hat{A}_i < 0$) suffer unbounded logit drift, crashing training mid-run.

Stage 4 · The 2025–2026 Surgical Fixes: Dr. GRPO, DAPO, MaxRL, CISPO, GSPO & R3

Every major RLVR algorithm of 2025–2026 targets one or more of GRPO's four pathologies:

  • Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., 2503.20783) — Fixing Pathology 1: Drops $\div\sigma_G$ ($\hat{A}_i = R_i - \bar{R}_G \equiv \frac{G-1}{G}\hat{A}_i^{\text{RLOO}}$) and replaces $\frac{1}{|y_i|}$ with a constant token normalizer $\frac{1}{M}$, restoring the exact unbiased sequence policy gradient.
  • MaxRL (2602.02710) & Pass@$k$ Training (2508.10751) — Fixing Pathology 1: Replaces GRPO's U-shaped weight $1/\sqrt{p_x(1-p_x)}$ with monotone decreasing weights $w(p_x) = \frac{1-(1-p_x)^T}{p_x} \to \frac{1}{p_x}$ (ascending $\sum_x \log p_x$) or $k(1-p_x)^{k-1}$ (ascending $\text{pass@}k$), up-weighting hard prompts ($p_x \to 0$) without amplifying easy ones ($p_x \to 1$).
  • DAPO (Yu et al., 2503.14476) — Fixing Pathologies 1, 2 & 3: Replaces symmetric clipping with Clip-Higher ($\varepsilon_{\text{low}} = 0.20,\; \varepsilon_{\text{high}} = 0.28$); filters out $\sum R_i \in \{0, G\}$ dead groups via Dynamic Sampling; switches to batch token-mean normalization $\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^G |y_j|}$; and drops the explicit KL penalty ($\beta = 0$).
  • CISPO (MiniMax-M1, 2506.13585) — Fixing Pathology 2 at High Batch Reuse ($\mu = 16$): Replaces upper zero-masking with a stop-gradient capped importance weight $\operatorname{sg}\!\big(\min(r_{i,t}, 1+\varepsilon_{\text{high}})\big)$ so rare reasoning tokens continue learning across all $\mu = 16$ micro-steps.
  • GSPO (2507.18071), TOPR (2503.14286), IcePop (2510.18855) & R3 (2510.11370) — Fixing Pathology 4: GSPO replaces noisy token ratios $r_{i,t}$ with the sequence geometric mean $s_i = (\prod_{t=1}^{|y_i|} r_{i,t})^{1/|y_i|}$; TOPR and Dual-Clip bound negative-advantage ($\hat{A}_i < 0$) ratios; IcePop masks tokens where the sampler-to-trainer ratio $\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{train}}/\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{roll}}$ exits $[0.5, 5.0]$; and R3 replays the inference engine's exact MoE expert assignments during backpropagation.

2.2 Factorizing the Evolution: The Master Estimator & the $r \equiv 1$ Collapse Theorem

Comparing the equations of Stage 1 through Stage 4 reveals that every policy-gradient algorithm in the literature computes the exact same master expression over a group of $G$ sampled trajectories $\{y_i\}_{i=1}^G$, differing only in three algebraic slots $(\hat{A}_{i,t},\; w_{i,t}\cdot m_{i,t},\; c_{i,t})$:

$$\hat{g}(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^{G} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \underbrace{c_{i,t}}_{\substack{\text{Slot 3: Aggregation}\\\text{(Dr.GRPO vs. GRPO/DAPO)}}} \cdot \underbrace{w_{i,t}(r_{i,t}) \cdot m_{i,t}(r_{i,t}, \hat{A}_{i,t})}_{\substack{\text{Slot 2: Trust-Region Weight & Gate}\\\text{(PPO / DAPO / CISPO / GSPO / SAPO)}}} \cdot \underbrace{\hat{A}_{i,t}}_{\substack{\text{Slot 1: Advantage & Scale}\\\text{(GAE / RLOO / GRPO / MaxRL / OPD)}}} \cdot \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t} \mid x, y_{i,<t})$$

Differentiating $\log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t} \mid s_{i,t})$ with respect to the pre-softmax logit vector $z_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|}$ gives the exact token-level force on vocabulary token $v \in \mathcal{V}$:

$$\frac{\partial \hat{\mathcal{J}}}{\partial z_{i,t,v}} = c_{i,t} \cdot w_{i,t}(r_{i,t}) \cdot m_{i,t}(r_{i,t}, \hat{A}_{i,t}) \cdot \hat{A}_{i,t} \cdot \Big(\mathbf{1}\{v = y_{i,t}\} - \pi_\theta(v \mid s_{i,t})\Big)$$

The Three Policies and Two Physical Gaps in Every Update. Three policy evaluations participate in every distributed RL step: (1) $\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{roll}}$, the rollout engine's policy (vLLM/SGLang, often FP8/BF16 with paged attention) that generated $y_i$; (2) $\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{train}}$, the trainer's policy evaluated once at $\theta_{\text{old}}$ before micro-step 1; and (3) $\pi_\theta^{\text{train}}$, the trainer's current policy at micro-step $k \in \{1, \dots, \mu\}$. Standard surrogates see only the trainer-side ratio $r_{i,t}(\theta)$, whereas the true off-policy ratio factors across two physical gaps:

$$r_{i,t}^{\text{true}}(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta^{\text{train}}(y_{i,t}\mid s_{i,t})}{\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{roll}}(y_{i,t}\mid s_{i,t})} = \underbrace{\frac{\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{train}}(y_{i,t} \mid s_{i,t})}{\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{roll}}(y_{i,t} \mid s_{i,t})}}_{\rho_{i,t}^{\text{roll}}\text{ (Gap 1: Engine Mismatch)}} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\pi_\theta^{\text{train}}(y_{i,t} \mid s_{i,t})}{\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{train}}(y_{i,t} \mid s_{i,t})}}_{r_{i,t}(\theta)\text{ (Gap 2: Policy Lag)}}$$

Proposition 1 (The $r \equiv 1$ On-Policy Collapse Identity). Suppose each rollout batch receives a single optimizer step ($\mu = 1$, zero asynchronous lag $\tau = 0$) and $\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{train}}$ is evaluated on the trainer at current parameters $\theta$. Then at the point of differentiation, $r_{i,t}(\theta) \equiv 1$ for every token $(i, t)$, and PPO-clip, GRPO, DAPO (Clip-Higher), Dual-Clip, CISPO, SAPO, TOPR, GSPO, and GMPO all return the exact same gradient once $\hat{A}_{i,t}$, $c_{i,t}$, and $\beta$ are matched:

$$\left.\hat{g}(\theta)\right|_{r_{i,t} \equiv 1} = \sum_{i=1}^{G} \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} c_{i,t} \, \hat{A}_{i,t} \, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t} \mid x, y_{i,<t})$$

Proof. At $r_{i,t} = 1$: $1 \in [1-\varepsilon_{\text{low}}, 1+\varepsilon_{\text{high}}]$ so $m_{i,t} = 1$ and $w_{i,t}(1) = 1$; $\min(1, 1+\varepsilon_{\text{high}}) = 1$ (CISPO); $4\sigma(0)(1-\sigma(0)) = 1$ (SAPO); and $s_i = 1^{1/|y_i|} = 1$ with $\left.\nabla_\theta s_i\right|_{r=1} = \frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_t \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid s_{i,t})$ (GSPO/GMPO). Note that if an explicit $k_3$ KL penalty is added outside the surrogate ($\beta > 0$ in original GRPO vs. $\beta = 0$ in DAPO/GSPO), its gradient $-\beta\big(\frac{\pi_{\text{ref}}}{\pi_\theta} - 1\big)\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta$ does not vanish at $r \equiv 1$ once $\pi_\theta \ne \pi_{\text{ref}}$. $\blacksquare$

Crucial Empirical Corollary (Why Published Surrogate Papers Still Report Large Differences): Auditing the primary PDFs and codebases of our 105-paper corpus reveals that zero headline surrogate papers actually run in the true on-policy regime B1 = On-Policy ($r \equiv 1 \implies \text{C1} = \varnothing$): DAPO (2503.14476) and MiniMax-M1 / CISPO (2506.13585) execute $\mu = 16$ gradient updates per rollout batch; DeepSeek-R1 (2501.12948) splits $8{,}192$ rollouts into $\mu = 16$ mini-batches; GMPO (2507.20673) runs $\mu = 8$ updates/round; GSPO (2507.18071) and SAPO (2511.20347) run $\mu = 4$ mini-batches/batch; and verl's default "synchronous" configuration (1024/256) performs $\mu = 4$ updates/batch. Even at $\mu = 1$, computing ratios against rollout-engine log-probabilities $\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{roll}}$ violates $r \equiv 1$ via Gap 1 (VeXact, 2605.14220 shows bitwise-exact MoE REINFORCE kept improving where the standard vLLM-based run degraded after $\sim 280$ steps, and R3, 2510.11370 shows $\sim 10\%$ of MoE routing decisions differ between training and inference on Qwen3-30B-A3B, affecting $94\%$ of sequences in at least one layer). Proposition 1 therefore proves that every reported accuracy gap between PPO, DAPO, CISPO, GSPO, and SAPO is an indirect measurement of off-policy batch reuse ($\mu \in [4, 16]$ in B1), un-replayed MoE routing drift (B1), or bundled changes to $\hat{A}_i$ ($\div\sigma_G$ in A2) and $c_{i,t}$ ($\frac{1}{|y_i|}$ vs. token-mean in A3).

2.3 From Three PG Slots to Five Code Modules—and Why We Need the Two-Layer Coordinate System ($\text{Target } \mathbf{A} \times \text{Regime } \mathbf{B} \times \text{Approximation } \mathbf{C} \to \text{Dynamics } \mathbf{D}$)

Step 1: Why the three policy-gradient slots expand to five code modules for indexing. The three slots $(\hat{A}_{i,t},\; w_{i,t}m_{i,t},\; c_{i,t})$ of Section 2.2 cover single-turn scalar-reward policy gradients (PPO, GRPO, Dr. GRPO, DAPO, CISPO, GSPO, SAPO). However, five families of post-training papers step outside that single-turn bandit box: (1) non-linear or game-theoretic objectives (pass@$k$ training $\sum_x (1-(1-p_x)^k)$, MaxRL $\sum_x \log p_x$, Nash-MD/SPPO equilibria); (2) alternative supervision signals (process reward models, LLM rubrics, intrinsic self-certainty/entropy, teacher log-probabilities in OPD); (3) non-PG update estimators (closed-form contrastive DPO/IPO/SimPO, rejection-sampled SFT like RAFT/ReST, value-based Q-learning); (4) multi-turn stateful credit assignment (agentic state grouping in GiGPO, environment-token masking in Search-R1); and (5) structured rollout sampling (difficulty curricula, prefix-tree branching in VinePPO/TreeRL). To index which module of a codebase any paper edits, the three slots naturally expand into five operational dimensions: Objective, Signal, Data, Estimator, and Update Control.

Step 2: Why a flat five-axis index cannot predict performance—the Two-Layer Split ($\pi^*$ Target vs. Approximation) and the $\beta = 0$ Simplex Subtlety. A flat five-axis index is useful as a lookup table for where a paper edited code, but treating those five dimensions as parallel peers loses the core causal hierarchy of RL: which dimensions determine where the policy converges (the stationary fixed point or optimal set $\pi^*$), and which dimensions determine how it gets there (bias, variance, speed, and stability)? Moreover, a flat five-axis table misses three critical facts: (i) the base model $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ sits on none of the five axes yet fixes the support set $\operatorname{supp}(\pi_{\text{ref}})$ and can flip an algorithm's sign across model families; (ii) the five axes are not orthogonal (advantage normalization is coded inside the Estimator but alters the cross-prompt Objective; Update Control is completely inert unless the Data axis is off-policy); and (iii) exploration dynamics (entropy collapse) form a feedback loop that determines which solutions remain samplable in finite time. Crucially, when $\beta = 0$ and $R(x,y)\in\{0,1\}$ (as in most modern RLVR recipes), $\pi^*$ degenerates from a single interior distribution into the simplex face $\Delta(\mathcal{Y}^+(x))$ of all distributions supported on correct responses ($p_x=1$); on that simplex face, Layer $\mathbf{C}$'s implicit mirror-descent path from $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ directly selects which valid solution modes survive, while prompt weighting $F(p_x)$ in A2 governs the finite-step cross-prompt gradient velocity ratio across easy vs. hard prompts.

Returning to the regularized optimal solution $\pi^*(y\mid x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\big(R(x,y)/\beta\big)$, we elevate the five code modules into a single Two-Layer Coordinate System ($\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}, \mathbf{D}$ conditioned on Cond) read directly from the canonical update equation

$$g(\theta) = \sum_x \underbrace{w(p_x)}_{\text{A2}} \sum_{i=1}^G \sum_{t=1}^{|y_i|} \underbrace{c_{i,t}}_{\text{A3}} \, \underbrace{\psi(r_{i,t}, \hat{A}_{i,t})}_{\text{C1, C2 (active iff B1 }\ne\text{ on-policy)}} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x, y_{i,<t}) - \underbrace{\beta\,\nabla_\theta \widehat{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta, \pi_{\text{ref}})}_{\text{A4}}$$

and one master diagnostic question: "Did this algorithmic modification change the fixed point $\pi^*$ (Layer A), or did it only alter the approximation path (Layer C) and entropy reachability (Layer D)?"

The Two Piecewise Falsifiable Predictive Rules of the Two-Layer Coordinate System:
• Rule 1 (Target Equivalence Inside the Stable Dynamical Interior; Phase Boundary Governed by Layer C): Under matched boundary conditions Cond (same $\pi_{\text{ref}}$, prompt distribution, and generation budget), any two algorithms sharing the same Target Layer coordinates (A1, A2, A3, A4) converge to the same asymptotic ceiling $A$ within the benchmark MDE noise band—provided Layer C remains inside the stable dynamical regime. When Layer C crosses a physical collapse boundary (such as unmasked Q-IV negative-logit drift in multi-turn agents, un-replayed MoE routing flips at $\mu \ge 4$, or length penalties under $L_{\text{cap}} < T^*(x)$), it directly collapses reachable support (D1) and lowers $A$.
• Rule 2 (Full-Coordinate Equivalence for the Training Curve): Under matched Cond and tuned effective step size C3, any two algorithms sharing the same (A, B, C) coordinates exhibit matching training trajectories $(A, B, \text{stability})$. Notice an important empirical hypothesis: in short-to-medium horizon math training (2506.21495, single seed), once data freshness B1 is set to online and A1–A4 are matched, Online DPO and GRPO fall into the same cell on MATH-500, suggesting "Estimator Family" matters far less than B1 data freshness—though whether this equivalence persists at 32k–64k long-CoT scale remains an open verification target.

Quadrant Classification of Post-Training Algorithms: What Each Algorithm Modifies and What Benefit It Brings

Rather than forcing a brittle one-dimensional "leaderboard ranking" across papers that operate under very different models, sequence lengths, and batch-reuse budgets, the clearest way to understand the post-training literature is through three orthogonal $2\times 2$ Quadrant Maps. Each algorithm is a surgical intervention designed for a specific quadrant, trading off one physical bottleneck for a concrete operational benefit:

Quadrant Map I · The $(r_{i,t} - 1,\; \hat{A}_{i,t})$ Trust-Region Plane (Off-Policy Ratio Drift $\times$ Advantage Sign). Every policy-gradient update multiplies the token score $\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid s_{i,t})$ by an effective scalar force $\psi(r_{i,t}, \hat{A}_{i,t}) = w(r_{i,t})\,m(r_{i,t}, \hat{A}_{i,t})\,\hat{A}_{i,t}$. Depending on whether the trajectory succeeded ($\hat{A}_{i,t} > 0$) or failed ($\hat{A}_{i,t} < 0$) and whether the token's probability has risen ($r_{i,t} > 1$) or fallen ($r_{i,t} < 1$) since rollout, every token update lands in one of four distinct physical quadrants:
  • Quadrant I ($\hat{A}_{i,t} > 0,\; r_{i,t} > 1$ — Positive Advantage, Probability Rising: The Exploration & Pivot Expansion Zone):
    What happens here: A token from a successful rollout is being reinforced across multiple micro-steps ($\mu > 1$). Because $r_{i,t} = \pi_\theta / \pi_{\text{old}}$, a multiplicative clip $r_{i,t} \le 1+\varepsilon$ restricts absolute probability growth to $\Delta\pi_{\max} = \varepsilon\,\pi_{\text{old}}$, meaning rare reasoning pivots ($\pi_{\text{old}} = 10^{-3}$) hit the clip wall after $\Delta\pi = 0.0002$ while high-probability tokens ($\pi_{\text{old}} = 0.5$) can shift by $0.10$.
    • PPO / Stock GRPO ($\psi = 0$ for $r > 1.20$): Hard-zeroes the gradient above $1.20$. Benefit: Maximum conservative stability—prevents any single positive batch from over-sharpening the policy or collapsing output diversity in one step.
    • DAPO Clip-Higher ($\psi = 0$ for $r > 1 + \varepsilon_{\text{high}} = 1.28$): Widens only the upper clip boundary from $1.20$ to $1.28$. Benefit: Gives low-probability reasoning and reflection tokens $40\%$ more multiplicative room to grow before cutoff, preventing premature entropy collapse on long-CoT tasks while keeping a hard safety cap.
    • CISPO ($\psi = \operatorname{sg}(\min(r, 1+\varepsilon_{\text{high}}))\hat{A}$): Replaces upper zero-masking with a stop-gradient constant cap $(1+\varepsilon_{\text{high}})\hat{A} > 0$. Benefit: Never discards a positive token across all $\mu = 16$ micro-steps—rare pivot tokens continue receiving a bounded positive update on every step, doubling sample-reuse efficiency.
    • TOPR ($\psi = \hat{A}$ for all $\hat{A} > 0$) & SAPO (smooth bell gate $\tau_{\text{pos}} = 1.0$): TOPR applies an unclipped SFT-like update on all positive trajectories; SAPO replaces the hard step discontinuity at $1+\varepsilon$ with a smooth sigmoid decay. Benefit: TOPR maximizes learning speed when positive rollouts are scarce (low pass@1); SAPO eliminates gradient cliffs at the clip boundary.
  • Quadrant II ($\hat{A}_{i,t} > 0,\; r_{i,t} < 1$ — Positive Advantage, Probability Falling: The Collateral Recovery Zone):
    What happens here: A token from a successful trajectory accidentally had its probability pushed down during earlier micro-steps on other sequences.
    • PPO / GRPO / DAPO ($\psi = r\hat{A}$ unclipped): Because $\min(r\hat{A}, (1-\varepsilon)\hat{A}) = r\hat{A}$ for $\hat{A}>0, r<1-\varepsilon$, the lower clip is inactive. Benefit: Allows the policy to actively pull back up good tokens that suffered collateral downward drift, while naturally attenuating the update by $r$ if the token has drifted far from $\pi_{\text{old}}$.
    • TOPR / Floor-Clamped CISPO ($\psi = \hat{A}$ or $\ge (1-\varepsilon_{\text{low}})\hat{A}$): Maintains a constant positive recovery force even if $r \ll 1$. Benefit: Ensures positive tokens are never lost even under heavy off-policy lag.
  • Quadrant III ($\hat{A}_{i,t} < 0,\; r_{i,t} > 1$ — Negative Advantage, Probability Rising: The Off-Policy Overshoot & Engine-Spike Zone):
    What happens here: A token from a failed trajectory currently has higher probability under $\pi_\theta^{\text{train}}$ than under $\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{roll}}$—either because collateral updates pushed it up, or because vLLM-to-trainer numerical drift (Gap 1) or MoE expert switching caused $\pi_{\text{old}}^{\text{roll}} \ll \pi_\theta^{\text{train}}$ ($r \gg 1$).
    • PPO / Stock GRPO / DAPO ($\psi = r\hat{A}$ unclipped): Because $\hat{A} < 0$, $\min(r\hat{A}, (1+\varepsilon)\hat{A}) = r\hat{A}$ is completely unclipped for $r > 1$. Benefit: In clean on-policy settings, aggressively corrects bad actions whose probability is mistakenly rising.
    • Dual-Clip PPO ($\psi = \max(r\hat{A}, c\hat{A}),\; c = 3$) & CISPO ($\psi = \operatorname{sg}(\min(r, 1+\varepsilon_{\text{high}}))\hat{A}$): Caps the maximum negative importance weight in Q-III at $c$ or $1+\varepsilon_{\text{high}}$ without zeroing the gradient. Benefit: Still pushes the rising bad token back down, but prevents $r \gg 1$ outlier spikes from exploding the gradient norm.
    • SAPO ($\tau_{\text{neg}} = 4.0 > \tau_{\text{pos}} = 1.0$), IcePop & R3 Routing Replay (B1 / C1): SAPO damps Q-III $4\times$ faster than Q-I; IcePop masks tokens where the sampler-to-trainer ratio $\rho^{\text{roll}}$ exits $[0.5, 5.0]$; R3 replays the rollout engine's exact MoE expert assignments in B1. Benefit: Eliminates hybrid-engine and MoE routing spikes at their source so Q-III never blows up.
  • Quadrant IV ($\hat{A}_{i,t} < 0,\; r_{i,t} < 1$ — Negative Advantage, Probability Already Suppressed: The Fluency & Prefix-Protection Zone):
    What happens here: A token from a failed rollout has already had its probability reduced ($r < 1$). Because $90\%$ of tokens in a failed CoT are normal syntax or valid intermediate steps, and because the logit score $\nabla_z \log \pi_\theta(y_t\mid s_t) = 1 - \pi_\theta(y_t\mid s_t) \to 1$ does not vanish as $\pi_\theta \to 0$, continuing to hammer Q-IV drives logits toward $-\infty$.
    • PPO / GRPO / DAPO ($\psi = 0$ for $r < 1-\varepsilon_{\text{low}} = 0.80$): Hard-masks the negative gradient once the token's probability has dropped by $20\%$. Benefit: Protects shared reasoning prefixes and model fluency across multiple epochs, preventing negative-logit collapse when failed rollouts dominate (e.g., hard prompts or multi-turn agentic tasks).
    • TOPR ($\psi = \operatorname{sg}(r)\hat{A}$ for $r \in (0, 1)$): Scales the negative gradient linearly by $r = \pi_\theta / \pi_{\text{old}} \to 0$. Benefit: Smoothly and automatically self-extinguishes as $\pi_\theta \to 0$ without requiring a hard $\varepsilon_{\text{low}}$ cutoff.
    • GSPO / GMPO (C1 = Sequence Geometric Ratio $s_i = (\prod_t r_{i,t})^{1/|y_i|}$): Applies the four-quadrant gate to the geometric-mean ratio of the whole trajectory rather than isolated tokens (computed in FP32 log-space in GMPO). Benefit: Prevents single-token MoE routing noise from triggering chaotic token-by-token masking; keeps the entire sequence update coherent with the sequence-level reward.

Quadrant Map II · The $(p_x,\; \mathcal{H}_t)$ Credit-Allocation Plane (Prompt Pass-Rate $p_x$ $\times$ Token Entropy $\mathcal{H}_t$). Orthogonal to the off-policy ratio $r_{i,t}$, algorithms differ in where they concentrate gradient budget across prompts of varying difficulty $p_x \in [0, 1]$ and tokens of varying branching uncertainty $\mathcal{H}_t = \mathcal{H}(\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s_t))$:
  • Q1 · Hard Frontier Prompts $\times$ High-Entropy Forking Tokens ($p_x \ll 0.5,\; \mathcal{H}_t$ High — The Capability Expansion Zone):
    What algorithms do here & why: MaxRL (2602.02710, A2 weight $w(p_x) \to 1/p_x$) and Pass@$k$ Policy Gradient (2508.10751) concentrate prompt weight on low-$p_x$ problems, while Token-Masking / Prefix-Tree Credit (SPO, 2505.23564; VinePPO, 2410.01679, C2) localizes updates to branching tokens (`"Wait"`, `"However"`, case splits) rather than uniform sequence credit. Benefit: Concentrates gradient capacity on the reasoning forks that determine whether a hard problem is solved, improving short-window efficiency and $\text{pass@}k$ frontier expansion.
  • Q2 · Hard Prompts $\times$ Low-Entropy Shared Prefix Tokens ($p_x \ll 0.5,\; \mathcal{H}_t \approx 0$ — The Prefix-Corruption Hazard Zone):
    What algorithms do here & why: Many tokens in a reasoning chain are deterministic algebraic steps or shared problem-setup prefixes ($\mathcal{H}_t \approx 0$). On a failed rollout, uniform sequence estimators penalize these correct shared tokens equally with the mistake token—causing DPO Likelihood Displacement and PG variance dilution. Step-DPO, Prefix-Tree Credit (VinePPO/SPO), and Token Masking (C2) zero out or localize gradients away from shared prefixes. Benefit: Prevents failed rollouts from unlearning valid intermediate algebra and syntax.
  • Q3 · Medium-to-Easy Prompts $\times$ Active Variance ($p_x \in [0.2, 0.9]$ — The Pass@1 Consolidation Zone):
    What algorithms do here & why: Dr. GRPO / RLOO (A2=Linear, A3=Const) weights prompts by natural variance $p_x(1-p_x)$ without length distortion, while Original GRPO divides by $\sigma_G = \sqrt{p_x(1-p_x)}$ (A2=Arcsin), boosting both $p_x \to 0$ and $p_x \to 1$. Benefit: Unbiased linear weighting (`Dr. GRPO / RLOO`) steadily consolidates $\text{pass@1}$ reliability without over-amplifying already-solved $p_x \to 1$ prompts or inflating wrong-answer lengths.
  • Q4 · Zero-Variance Dead Prompts ($p_x \in \{0, 1\}$, i.e. $\sum_{i=1}^G R_i \in \{0, G\}$ — The Zero-Bit Zone):
    What algorithms do here & why: When all $G$ rollouts succeed or all $G$ fail, binary outcome RLVR (A1=Verifier) yields $\hat{A}_i \equiv 0$. DAPO Dynamic Sampling and ScaleRL Difficulty Curriculum (D2) filter out $p_x \in \{0, 1\}$ prompts before backward pass; On-Policy Distillation (OPD, A1=Teacher Logprobs) replaces the $0$-bit outcome reward with dense teacher logits $\log \pi_{\text{teacher}}(y_t\mid s_t)$ on every token. Benefit: Dynamic filtering saves wasted backward updates without changing $\pi^*$; OPD turns $p_x = 0$ dead prompts into high-bandwidth learning signals.

2.4 Actionable Engineering Tiering Derived from the Two-Layer Structure

The three practical engineering categories follow directly from the Two-Layer Sufficiency Coordinate System ($\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B}, \mathbf{C}, \mathbf{D}$) and the Master Fixed-Point Test (Figure 3):

  • Category I · Fixing Approximation Correctness (Chapter 4): Non-negotiable repairs in A4, B1, C2 that prevent the approximation layer from silently drifting away from the intended fixed point $\pi^*$.
  • Category II · Conditional Off-Policy & Throughput Stabilizers (Chapter 5): Ratio gates and staleness controls in C1, C2 that are empty at B1 = On-Policy ($r \equiv 1 \implies \text{C1} = \varnothing$) but maintain valid convergence when system constraints force B1 into off-policy regimes ($\mu > 1, \tau > 0$, or MoE engine drift).
  • Category III · True Target-Layer $\pi^*$ Shifts & Sample-Efficiency Multipliers (Chapters 6–8): Explicit or disguised modifications to the stationary fixed point A1–A4 (plus boundary conditions Cond and D2 active yield). Any "new loss" that does not alter A1–A4 or prevent D1 entropy collapse can only move convergence speed $B$, never the true capability ceiling $A$.
